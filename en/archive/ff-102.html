⛱️ ff.102.1 The future doesn't go on holiday

Substack ↗

Matt Clancy's newsletter (an innovation-and-society researcher at Iowa University) is called What’s New Under the Sun.

A sun now at the equinox of this summer of 2024.

The perfect name for the last summer newsletter, then, because progress doesn't go on holiday. Or does it?

Matt shows how, since the 80s, the words “progress” and “future” have become less and less frequent, replaced by risk and caution.

If you remove “future” from the synonyms of progress, the trend is even more obvious.

Mea culpa. By christening progress “futuro fortissimo”, I'm only messing up his analysis.

Below, two eye-opening interviews. Click the links for a fortissimo summary.

🚕 ff.102.2 Wuhan: from Covid to Baidu

Substack ↗

As you'll have guessed, we're talking about positivity. Not for COVID, even though I'm taking you somewhere well known.

In Wuhan, 3 taxis in 100 are Baidu robotaxis. For 10 km, 4 yuan ($0.55), 1/5 of the cost of a conventional taxi. The fleet, currently 500 vehicles, will double by the end of the year.

In Europe too: Wayve respecting a cyclist in London.

By 2035 autonomous taxis are expected to grow 10x, reaching revenues comparable to Apple's and bringing us cities with more liveable spaces (🚦 ff.34.3 Rethinking cities: liveable spaces), fewer accidents and more free time.

🔭 ff.102.3 Automating scientific research

Substack ↗

Founded in Tokyo by ex-Google employees, Sakana AI has unveiled its AI Scientist: a system to automate scientific discovery that generates ideas, runs experiments and writes scientific papers.

At just $15 per scientific paper.

🩸 ff.102.4 From blood to Amazon

Substack ↗

Let's carry on with our roundup of autonomous systems. Did somebody say drones?

Zipline, founded in 2016 to deliver blood in Rwanda and going on to cut post-partum deaths by 50%, is aiming to expand.

For now, it handles a number of deliveries equal to 0.1% of Amazon's. But it does it faster and with fewer emissions.

The CEO dreams of rental items delivered instantly, a mix between Amazon Prime and Vinted.

The future I'd like. A weekend of gardening and videography in the countryside.

With one click, I get garden tools, a professional camera and a video-editing computer delivered right on the spot.

When the stay is over, I give it all back. No cluttering the house with things I rarely use.

In short, more dematerialisation. But not of pizza.
