💊 ff.104.1 The body hacker

Substack ↗

We're seeing growing interest in longevity, supplements and diets. Or at least, the algorithms filtering my internet are.

And so futuro fortissimo veers a little into all that.

For example, I stumbled upon Dave Asprey. A peculiar, controversial character, so much so that the British Dietetic Association described the diet he proposes as pseudo-scientific.

In his book Smarter Not Harder he proposes biohacking. His theory is that we often “stress” the body with insane workouts, in intensity and duration, without feeding it the food it needs to reap the benefits.

For example, through mineral deficiency (45% of Americans don't reach their daily magnesium)

In the book, Dave lists supplements, broken down by whether you want to optimise athletic performance, longevity or muscle mass.

And more. He gives the dosages. Crucial.

Nerd corner. A guide to increasing your mineral intake from food.

🦸‍♂️ ff.104.2 The Fantastic 4

Substack ↗

I know. We're stressed and in a rush, pushed by social compromises into less-than-ideal food choices too.

Here are my fantastic 4, super-supplements to correct course a little.

Electrolytes Even eating well, hitting your magnesium and potassium needs isn't trivial. Especially in summer, when we sweat a bit more. And indeed Polase is on everyone's lips this season (and on Esselunga's shelves). The problem? As I said, the dosages. Let's look at an example: Soy strips Eating protein and fibre while minimising fat? Almost impossible. When I discovered texturized vegetable protein (the name is scary, but it's soy) I was amazed: €1.5 per 100g, delivering 50 g of protein, 15 g of fibre and only 7g of fat. Glycine Glycine supports longevity by aiding collagen turnover and boosting antioxidant defences (glutathione synthesis). We know that cutting a single protein, methionine (found in animal protein), promotes longevity. Glycine balances methionine's potential negative effects (for longevity). Siim Land, top 10 in the anti-ageing olympics, suggests a dose of 12-18 grams a day. Wheat germ In 🌶️ ff.99.2 Polenta and spermidine we discovered another molecule with a mystical name: spermidine extends life. 10 g of wheat germ (mixed into bread dough or morning porridge) is a good base. Then mango, soy and polenta!

🤮 ff.104.3 The Abominable 5

Substack ↗

The best thing about life? To improve, you don't necessarily have to do stratospheric things. You just have to not do the wrong ones.

So here are 5 foods or ingredients to avoid. Always.

Margarine (trans fats) Of all fats, avoid trans fats above all: they raise the risk of heart disease and visceral inflammation by 30% (🍔ff.94.2 Good fats and bad fats). Processed puddings and yoghurts (polysorbate 80) This single emulsifier can reduce gut-barrier integrity by 50%, raising the risk of systemic inflammation. Jarred mayonnaise (oxidised seed oils on supermarket shelves) Commercial mayonnaise contains seed oils, which oxidise during their long stay in the jar. These oxidised seed oils increase inflammation and fat mass. Fizzy drinks (corn syrup) Consumption of high-fructose corn syrup is linked to a 20% drop in learning and memory abilities. Froot Loops (Red Dye 40) Here too, a single ingredient makes the difference: Red Dye 40 is associated with a 10% increase in ADHD (Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder) in children and with markers of neuroinflammation in adults.

👩🏻‍⚕️ ff.104.4 GoodGPT: my private nutritionist

Substack ↗

Wait, wait, wait. You'll ask me: how can I know all this? You don't need to. Just follow the rule of simple, of less: fewer ingredients on the label, fewer processed foods, fewer chances of swallowing risky ingredients.

To help you, I built a GPT to guide your everyday choices.

It's called BuonGPT (bon appétit!). It starts from the simple idea of ranking foods. Based on nutritional values (relative to the kcal they provide), it tells you how “dense” in good stuff a food is.

It can also compare two foods, calorie for calorie, to tell you which to prefer, and you can try it here.
