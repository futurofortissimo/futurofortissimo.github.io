👩 ff.109.1 LinkedIn's most influential woman

Substack ↗

Arianna Huffington (founder of the newspaper that bears her name) is the woman with the most followers on LinkedIn.

I discovered her in a recent podcast with Peter Diamandis (creator of the XPRIZE, the Nobel for technology that for 30 years has offered millions to whoever develops innovative technologies).

Arianna's story fascinated me: at the Huffington Post she tried to overturn the idea of sensationalist, negatively viral media by introducing the What's Working section, news centred on the positive.

An idealism shattered by the media business model, focused on maximising likes and engagement.

🌱 ff.109.2 From media to digital wellbeing

Substack ↗

So she launched Thrive Global, to channel the power of algorithms with one priority: human psychophysical wellbeing.

The mountain comes to Muhammad. Integrated into Microsoft Teams, Slack and similar digital spaces, Thrive brings a release valve “to the workplace”, like meditation, mood check-ins and digital detox.

🧶 ff.109.3 Untangling a ball of yarn in a minute

Substack ↗

Ariadne's thread. Let's look at an example of how Thrive uses AI to get out of the labyrinth of an algorithmic society. In one minute.

Control techniques like eye tracking to gauge a user's engagement are already widely used for commercial purposes; Arianna wants to use the same ones to gauge - during a meeting - an employee's stress level.

Just give me a minute A breath of air One more moment Pooh, Dammi solo un minuto

Thrive has a Reset feature: one minute with a user's favourite song, messages and images, to clear your head between one Zoom meeting and the next.

Or a password will do: Mauricio Estrella got over the depression of a betrayal and divorce by resetting his PC password to “Forgive@h3r”. He turned a boring work chore into a mantra, a moment of meditation and acceptance.

💲 ff.109.4 Alms from AI

Substack ↗

And if I share with you my story would you share your dollar with me? Aloe Blacc, I Need A Dollar

Our society is increasingly automated, algorithmic. It measures us and recommends purchases, diets, workouts. Human free will is cancelled out.

But, but, but. According to Harari, the problem isn't spending on artificial intelligence, but the failure to invest in human consciousness (Google spends more on data centres than on employee salaries, 🦖ff.82.1 Mass extinction?)

So Arianna's project is heading in the right direction: building algorithms to improve our self-awareness. It's encouraging that OpenAI's Sam Altman is among Thrive Global's investors.

Is this the right ball of yarn?
