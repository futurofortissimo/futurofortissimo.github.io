1️ ff.10.1 Will we really live only in another reality?

Substack ↗

This was the first question I was asked. In my view, the metaverse will be made up of two realities:

A totalising one (VR), immersive and disconnected from the reality around us. This will be like today's video games, but with more powerful technology (well-made 3D headsets, pressure sensors, taste simulators…). An augmented one (AR), integrated with reality. For example, walking around the city, we could see shop windows with special effects, or monuments perfectly recreated in their original state, or even see our surroundings in any season (that way we'd be sure to catch Tokyo's cherry blossoms).

What % of time we'll spend in one reality or the other is hard to say. But the metaverse is that continuum that will connect digital objects more and more.

2️ ff.10.2 Are we already in the metaverse?

Substack ↗

But then again, aren't we already, a little, in the metaverse?

Recent data shows we spend about a quarter of our day on our smartphones (an hour more than in 2019). If we add the time we spend in front of other screens, I think the answer is pretty obvious.

3️ ff.10.3 Will we only buy digital homes?

Substack ↗

I was also asked whether people will buy digital homes to live in. I think it's very possible. After all, some people are spending that kind of money on NFTs of an image.

Flexing in front of the world: one reason is that digital has no borders. Take home furnishings: if I buy a contemporary artwork, at most I can show it off at dinners or events among acquaintances; in a digital world this status can potentially be shared with 10^6 more people. Imagine how that can scale.

Transferable objects: virtual objects, just like digital people, must however be integrated into a single protocol or into easily interchangeable languages. For example, if I produce a text or an image, I can easily post it on Instagram as well as on Twitter. In the metaverse/web3 I must be able to show off an object easily anywhere (in Fortnite or during a work meeting on Teams).

4️ ff.10.4 Will we get paid to shoot?

Substack ↗

One thing that fascinates me about the metaverse is the possible integration of cryptocurrencies, or tokens, able to "pay back the nodes of the network itself". Each platform could give users a currency based on participation or performance, to motivate them and make them feel part of the project.

For example: Call of Duty is what it is because so many people play it. To increase the time spent on the platform, it could be interesting to create a digital currency tied to the experience points, challenges and days of play a player has accumulated. Obviously, one that can then be spent or transferred to other games (like on Diablo!).

Another example: I bought one of Pinguini Tattici Nucleari's first CDs, way back in 2010. In a blockchain-based world, this could have guaranteed me a (tiny) share of "ownership" in a band I strongly believed in, and then let me enjoy the benefits when that band ended up at Sanremo.

Helium, which asks users to share their wifi and pays them in tokens, is a concrete example of such possibilities.

5️ ff.10.5 The world of video games: vanguard or stagnation?

Substack ↗

Microsoft's acquisition is truly crazy. 69 billion (more than half the company's cash) and it far exceeds what it paid for LinkedIn, Skype, Github and Nokia (combined).

But, but, but. The mobile gaming market is twice the size of the console market, and growing faster (13% vs 7%).

And yet Zynga, the company behind FarmVille, was sold for 13 billion a few weeks ago. Isn't 69 billion excessive? Maybe, but this way Microsoft is:

Monopolising digital realities (where people connect to write code, to build business and connections, to have fun) with the ability to integrate them easily; Concentrating know-how on technologies (like 3D rendering) that will be fundamental.

But is the video game world keeping up with the times?

It's the vanguard of rendering, alternative realities and digital communities (as I argued talking about Discord in ff.3, now widely used in the crypto world). It stagnates on outdated business models, without much inventiveness (just look at how the Call of Duty, Fifa and other sagas have been recycling the same mechanics for more than ten years). Plus, it monetises little compared to its potential.
