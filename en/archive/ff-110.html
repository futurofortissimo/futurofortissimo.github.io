🏛️ ff.110.1 All roads lead to Rome's potholes

Substack ↗

For two months, my home near Bergamo has been cut off. A hairpin bend gave way after heavy rain, closing the only road to Val Seriana.

The most optimistic forecasts put the end of works at late 2025. AGI might arrive first.

That bend is yet another example of how fragile Italian infrastructure is. World-class fragile.

In life as in infrastructure, new is more exciting: a new bike, a new outfit, a new Calatrava bridge. Going to the mechanic, the tailor, or calling the builder for a leak is far less glamorous, especially in the throwaway era.

But with infrastructure, we have to make the most of what already exists. Especially bridges. The Morandi in Genoa and the banknotes in our wallets remind us of that.

🚧 ff.110.2 An endless construction site

Substack ↗

And yet the State struggles to fund maintenance of what's been built. Extreme weather events like the Valencia rains don't help.

And so around us there's an endless construction site, overseen by retired old men.

All that's left is to make a show of it, Big Brother style: the livestream of the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation's I-95 site.

The truth is we're in the middle of a productivity cliff. McKinsey points it out too: construction productivity has been stuck since the 2000s.

And yet America used to be efficient: 138 days after the idea, Apollo 8 reached the Moon.

💎 ff.110.3 Cryptocurrencies versus state obsolescence

Substack ↗

The US elections sent a clear message, however hard to swallow: the State is too slow, regulation suffocating.

SpaceX lands rockets again, Starlink brings internet where fibre doesn't reach. No coincidence that Musk himself proposes - with his usual irony - DOGE, the Department of Government Efficiency.

Trump's election and Bitcoin's spike: from 69,000 to 75,000 dollars in a few hours. A sign that we're moving beyond modern democracy? Are we about to 🎼 ff.52 Found a Nation in your garage?

According to Naval, yes: Bitcoin at all-time highs is the first sign of relief from the weight of the State, which gets heavier every day.

Meanwhile, remember that El Salvador's president has been buying one Bitcoin a day since November 2022: the strategic reserve is worth 600 million dollars today (at the start of 2024 it was worth 100).

But, but, but. Maybe decentralised democracy is an illusion too.

🤤 ff.110.4 Robots and AI: opportunities

Substack ↗

There is an alternative to digital anarchy: the State can save itself with the help of artificial intelligence and robots.

Sidewalk AI (Carlo Ratti, MIT Senseable City Lab) uses AI to map the condition of pavements.

The other technological piece is robots: they could speed up construction sites, cutting costs and boosting efficiency. On that note, the update on the collaboration between Figure AI and BMW.

While we wait for the robot revolution, all we can do is fix reality following the Japanese principle of Kintsugi (金継ぎ), or Kintsukuroi (金繕い): potholes turned into works of art by Ememem.
