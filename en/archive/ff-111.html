🍊 ff.111.1 A life to squeeze

Substack ↗

On futuro fortissimo we also talk about time. We've converted it into bananas (⚖️ ff.61.1 Measuring pollution in hours of life) and slowed it down, noticing the variations in everyday life (📝 ff.65.3 Homework for life).

The end of the year is perfect for stopping, and stopping time. As I do with a recap video on YouTube, for whoever's left of the 50,000 subscribers who used to watch me shoot things up in Call of Duty.

Stopping isn't enough, though. First we have to turn our life energy, our work, into life: reckless, remembered.

💯ff.111.2 A reckless life

Substack ↗

I want a reckless life I want a life like the ones in the movies I want an over-the-top life I want a life like Steve McQueen Vasco Rossi, Vita Spericolata

You don't need to be Vasco to live fully, you just need a method: Bill Perkins's, an engineer turned gas trader.

His book Die with Zero opens with a thought: if we die with €10,000 in the bank, we've lost 3 months of life at work; missing out on 5 trips, 50 dinners or 3 months of retirement.

🛡️ ff.111.3 Children, health and wasted years

Substack ↗

Bill asks us to “die with zero”. Clearly an impossible provocation (obviously nobody knows the day they'll die). But it reveals hidden inefficiencies in how we manage our finances:

Inheritance: children receive it at 60, when they're already financially settled. Better to help them between 25 and 35, when they can use it for a home, a business, a family or more experiences. Health: in case of serious illness, savings can't cover huge medical bills (especially in America). Better a good health insurance policy. Retirement: our estimates of post-work years are rough. Better life annuities.

📈 ff.111.4 Experiences = investments

Substack ↗

Scandalous? With his hedonism, Bill seems to trample values like family, health and security for the future: to all intents and purposes a mental stumbling block (skàndalon), a stone on the main road.

A scandal first of all for himself, since Bill wants to remember the mental traps he fell into when young, saving €1,000 of a €16,000 salary. When his boss found out, he told him: "Are you an idiot? You're sacrificing 1/16 (22 days a year) of your life for money you'll make in 3 days in 10 years' time!".

So let's see how Bill, through an analogy between money, time and investments, makes us rethink life.

Memories as dividends? In finance, the earlier you invest, the more dividends you accumulate. The same goes for experiences: a trip to Italy at 20 generates 20+ more years of memories, lessons and knowledge than the same trip at 40.

Physical inflation. A dollar spent at 60 buys less life. Bill's example is a day on the slopes. In old age, physically slower, we do fewer runs, fewer experiences.

But, but, but. Fascinating as I find Bill's ideas, allow me some criticism:

without novelty, more experiences don't turn into more moments to remember (🆕 ff.65.1 Time = new experiences?) novelty depends on our attention (or distraction), regardless of how much happens around us; old age, even if it slows us down physically, brings out the value of things we took for granted when young

In short. A fine mess. A big bang. Happy end, better beginning.

P.S. Exercise for the 31st: maximise the next 365/22,000 days, in case you die (with zero) at 60.
