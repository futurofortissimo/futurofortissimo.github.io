💸 ff.112.1 5 years to make money

Substack ↗

The start of the year means resolutions and predictions. Both doomed to crumble against the wall of an ever more unpredictable reality.

The COVID explosion taught us that our linear minds don't grasp exponential trends, says Neil deGrasse Tyson.

An example: o3, OpenAI's latest model, doubled its (already staggering) accuracy in a few months.

Faced with this acceleration, is Raoul Pal right to say that within 5 (6) years the current socio-financial system will completely lose its meaning/value?

🙅 ff.112.2 We can't predict anything

Substack ↗

Putting a face on it? We don't understand reality, not just the future one but the present: Hollywood is scared of generative art recreating actors, yet half of box-office takings are already tied to non-human characters.

Instead of making predictions, Ved Sen invites us to replace “never” with “what if”.

Here are my 5 very personal what-ifs:

What if the robot population overtakes the human one? What if I live to 500? If I can't predict the next 5 years, how do I think about the next 100? What if a swarm of autonomous drones appeared on the horizon? If energy and money lose value, will biological health (measured in VO2max) be the new wealth?

🏋️ ff.112.3 We can only prepare as well as possible

Substack ↗

Given an uncertain future, all we can do is refocus on the present, keeping good habits and making small investments.

I've kept a personal diary for 10 years. I thought it would be useless, given the jumble of text I'd hardly ever reread. This year, thanks to Google's NotebookLM, I turned it into a podcast episode about my life.

Hearing two “people” comment, not without some irony, on my very personal worries made me smile. Now I can't wait to generate a whole film of my life.

🏞️ ff.112.4 Unexpected rivers

Substack ↗

The diary is just one example of how AI (we'll soon talk about Google Deep Research) processes the jumble of information we generate (♾️ ff.72.1 Digital infinity).

Research on the link between drinking-water hardness and lower cardiovascular risk has existed since the '60s. Only recently have we managed to rediscover and understand it more deeply.

A small example: I took the data on the mineral salts in Bergamo's water and, with a simple copy-paste into ChatGPT, generated a report on mineral salts in the water of various towns in the province, noticing that my village tops the chart for magnesium. Hooray!

💧 ff.112.5 Water everywhere

Substack ↗

2025 isn't a blank slate, but the continuation of life's flow. So let's go back to the recent invitation to follow the Tao, to be water (☯️ ff.108.3 Be water).

Water that flows without resolutions or predictions, but with a direction: downwards. Following a single law: gravity.

Without knowing whether it'll end up in a sea, a lake, a toilet, a fruit or your mouth.
