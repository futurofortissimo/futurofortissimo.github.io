👨🏻‍🏫 ff.115.1 A lesson with a billionaire

Substack ↗

I'm in China right now, and Chinese is DeepSeek, the open source model that makes free what OpenAI offered for €200/month (🍓 ff.103.1 Think before you speak).

The serpent of knowledge has slipped out.

Slytherin or evergreen? More the latter, since Chris Sacca (the 2,378th richest person in the world) backs climate-change solutions through Lowercarbon Capital (some portfolio companies):

Carbon Engineering captures CO2 from the atmosphere and is trying to bring the cost down to $100/tonne, the critical level for offsetting the social cost of emissions. Lilac Solutions extracts lithium from brine, with methods 3x better than current ones. Solugen uses enzymes to convert sugars into chemicals (among CNBC's 50 most disruptive companies of 2024).

He was recently on Tim Ferriss's show. Let's see what they talked about.

❔ ff.115.2 What to study with ChatGPT around?

Substack ↗

As a good investor, Chris Sacca follows tech trends closely. With podcasts? No. By talking to ChatGPT for hours (after asking it to impersonate Buckminster Fuller, 🎼 ff.48 Resurrecting Aristotle).

Better Call Saul and Mr. Robot. It's no accident that the protagonists of these series, magnetic personalities, are a lawyer and a programmer: symbols of two professions any sane parent would have suggested to their kids. Until a year ago.

Today - in the age of AI - the best-paid jobs (actors, programmers and lawyers) are also the most at risk (you don't need McKinsey to tell you that).

We've already got used to it, but until recently coding the game Snake took years of study. Today DeepSeek does it bug-free from 6 words: “write the snake game in python”.

🐍 ff.115.3 Exponential venoms

Substack ↗

Speaking of snakes. Artificial intelligence is slithering out of chatbots and into practical applications.

ProteinMNPP is a “ChatGPT” trained on protein structures (instead of internet text, ⚛️ff.98.4 Quantum systems). In Nature, a study shows how to find an antidote to snake venom in seconds instead of months.

From here, a reflection on human short-sightedness about exponentials, featuring the illustration by Tim Urban, who in 2015 wrote about the Artificial Intelligence revolution.

Chris points out that these trends are everywhere: he claims nuclear fusion (not fission!) is being achieved daily (with Q>1), precisely by using AI (☢️ ff.28.1 Artificial intelligence for nuclear fusion).

How do we avoid being squeezed by this boa of technology? We'll see in 10 days. Now back to eating bao.
