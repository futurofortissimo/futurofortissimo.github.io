Spices between the internet and Brave New World

🌎 ff.118.1 Brave New World

Substack ↗

Almost everyone knows the book 1984, Orwell and Big Brother: controlling society by controlling information, through censorship.

But what if the “new world” were instead the one of Brave New World?

Back in 1932 Aldous Huxley painted another dystopian society: one where a “spice” mentally controls the population.

Here's the alternative to censorship: drown the truth in a chaos of content (AI-generated too); throw it back into the “simulacrum of stimuli” the brain has to make sense of (👨‍💻 ff.64.4 Matrix and matter).

A spice, a drug: that of the media, of TikTok feeds we can't take our eyes off, modern gladiators in the Colosseum (Juvenal's “panem et circenses” on steroids).

🌶️ ff.118.2 Paprika - Dreaming a dream パプリカ (2006)

Substack ↗

Paprika (a spice) is the title of Satoshi Kon's masterpiece film, which I watched recently.

It connects other dots from last issue's mental journey, 🎼 ff.117 We live in a simulation.

The director portrays the danger of a digital world (but also modern society) in which everything can coexist: the internet (but also a skyscraper) where every tab (or floor) is a possibility.

Looking for a loo in Hong Kong, I walked into a skyscraper. At random, I pressed 17 in the lift. When the doors opened, my lungs were flooded with eugenol: I was on a floor full of nothing but dentists.

It was yet another confirmation of the non-linear, electronic, quantum reality we live in. Where parallel realities coexist, stacked like the floors of a skyscraper.

Here's the desert - a space with no landmarks - painted in Paprika, where all our myths and symbols parade past.

A desert of signs with no single meaning: umbrellas, for rain or for sun?

🌳 ff.118.3 The cure: trees and silences?

Substack ↗

How do you “unplug” from this drug, this spice?

In Hong Kong, I reacted by running a marathon.

Not so much to cover the infamous 42.196 km, or for a medal; but to find the time I needed to get bored again.

To switch off from the city's constant stimuli (one giant social network) and listen to what was happening inside me.

In short, to find my “groundedness” again, the roots and fixed points of my being: running, podcasts and simple food (bananas).

And to listen again to the songs that matter most.

Where are you going, tree? So high That every shoot is less firmly rooted And every leaf is weaker And bound to fall just a little above the roots And all these arms flung open to the sky And those slender fingers, because they're fighting this gravity Because they're fighting this gravity Na na, against this gravity, na na Eugenio in Via di Gioia, Albero

And in all this music, to find silence again. Like John Cage's 273 seconds (also degrees Kelvin).
