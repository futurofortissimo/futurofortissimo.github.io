🏃🏼‍♂️ ff.120.1 How much running to “outrun” death?

Substack ↗

Our future runs through health, movement and sport. That's why these topics are at home here.

How I METs your mother. In 🎼 ff.86 Burning oxygen is life we introduced the Metabolic Equivalent, a unit for measuring movement (basal metabolism = 1 MET).

It works even if you run a marathon in space:

runneralerts A post shared by @runneralerts

Using the Bruce test, a study sorted 750,000 people by their metabolic fitness (ranking them by the METs they could sustain).

Nerd corner. 10 METs equals running 10 km in an hour (6 min/km); the relationship between the Cooper test, VO2max and METs.

Back to the study: after 20 years, those who could sustain 14 METs died 4 times less than those who stopped at 5.

💕 ff.120.3 Matters of the heart

Substack ↗

Candy from strangers? If not burned off, blood sugar makes your vessels stiffer (like a Maillard reaction: Advanced Glycation Endproducts, AGEs).

That explains the link between sugar, blood glucose and heart problems.

When activated, muscles act like a sponge and “soak up the excess”. That's why VO2Max (a measure of oxygen and sugar consumption) is a longevity marker.

🍼 ff.120.4 Lactate for the brain?

Substack ↗

Keeping arterial stiffening in check is one of the benefits of movement.

Moving also produces lactic acid (the stuff that turns stairs into our worst enemy the day after a mountain hike).

Mental energy. Yes, the brain burns 20% of the sugar we take in. But did you know that neurons prefer lactate? So much so that it's used to treat brain injuries.

So the lactic acid produced during sport promotes the growth of new nerve cells (sport and Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor, BDNF).

⚔️ ff.120.5 Tumour vs heartbeats?

Substack ↗

Quick bonus. It seems the “high heart rate” of exercise can break apart some metastatic tumour cells.

Not all of them are sensitive to these “circulatory forces”. But exercise can also help normalise blood flow to tumour cells, improving the therapies under way.
