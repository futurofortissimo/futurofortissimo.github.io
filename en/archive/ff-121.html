🌊 ff.121.1 Flow basics

Substack ↗

I first came across the idea of flow a few years ago, reading Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi: it's that mental state of total immersion in an activity that makes us lose our sense of self and of time.

What encourages flow? Physical movement, risk, the complexity of a task (but not too much: the 4% rule), novelty, the absence of distractions.

When we play, we often slip into flow. No coincidence that it was first measured in chess players, with an increase in theta waves and activation of the "implicit system".

McKinsey: managers in flow are five times more productive. DARPA (the US defence agency): military snipers acquiring targets 2 times faster. Red Bull: the North America's High-Performance Center led by Leslie Sherlin

♟️ ff.122.3 From chess to Patagonia

Substack ↗

I thought the benefits were only mental, so I was surprised to find flow as the explanation for extreme sports.

Faced with a real chance of dying (ultimate human performance), flow is always there, as Steven Kotler points out in The Rise of Superman.

And it opens the doors to the impossible:

Tony Hawk, skateboarding, a trick with a 900° rotation. Tyler Bradt, kayaking, a 58-metre drop. Dean Potter, climbing, inventing the free solo (he later died in 2015 BASE-jumping, RIP 🕊️), scaling Fitz Roy in six and a half hours. The new ChatGPT is phenomenal at creating images (but not perfect yet)

🔺 ff.122.4 Pyramids in 4 minutes?

Substack ↗

Not even an Ironman is enough any more: Mitchell Hutchcraft's Project Limitless, a 12,000-kilometre triathlon (34 km across the English Channel, 10,000 km by bike from France to India and, to finish, a light trek in Nepal up to Everest).

runnersworldita A post shared by @runnersworldita

Why this explosion of flow and extremes? Why do we keep raising the bar?

Behind it is Maslow's pyramid: once survival and food are guaranteed, we need stimuli to give our days meaning (🔺 ff.62.4 The pyramid inside all of us).

The Bannister effect. Once something is shown to be doable, loads of people start doing it (good), but the new limit moves to a higher level of risk (bad).
