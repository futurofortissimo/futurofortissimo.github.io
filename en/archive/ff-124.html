🦴 ff.124.1 Dalí melts collarbones

Substack ↗

I fell off my bike. Or rather, someone made me fall. Result: 30 days of forced sofa time and an AI deep-dive I hadn't planned.

In 🎼 ff.109 A family doctor called AI we talked about how ChatGPT isn't just for funny pictures or automated emails, but can make a real difference in everyday life.

This injury confirmed it.

First of all, it helped me laugh it off. Collarbones melting like Dalí's clocks, a post-trauma selfie turned into an emoji and a cubist sketch.

✍️ ff.124.2 Longa manus

Substack ↗

We've also seen that AI is a “longa manus”, autonomous and able to do work for us (🎼 ff.119 Hands everywhere).

Since typing is impossible for me right now, it helped me shape this newsletter, transcribing my voice notes and offering style edits.

For Neuralink's third patient (paralysed by ALS) this is the only way to post on X.

Bonus. Neuroprostheses turn thoughts into speech in real time, up to 90 words a minute.

🩺 ff.124.3 A family doctor (in your pocket)

Substack ↗

There are more examples online of artificial intelligence solving physical, real-world health problems. So much so that Reid Hoffman, co-founder of LinkedIn, talks about superagency:

In Italy we can still count on solid public healthcare, even if it's under growing pressure. But imagine living in Nigeria or Argentina, with little money and no access to expert doctors.

Or, even with the means, not finding anyone who really understands what's wrong with you (like the Y Combinator founder who beat her migraines after years).

AI is democratising and easing access to all of this.

So, during this convalescence, ChatGPT (o3) became my personal radiologist (and 10 days after the fall it analysed a video of a movement I was doing and advised me not to repeat it, as it was far too soon!).

🤹 ff.124.4 ChatGPT, jack of all trades

Substack ↗

It also guided me through physio exercises, built a recovery plan for my Ironman in September (fingers crossed) and delivered a PhD-style review on using collagen + vitamin C to support bone regrowth.

In short, ChatGPT may not be the best doctor in the world, but it's often better than the one available: stressed, rushed, with 5 minutes for us after a 12-hour shift.

And how are you using this superagency? Tell me in the comments!
