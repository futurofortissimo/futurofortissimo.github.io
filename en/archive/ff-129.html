🕳️ff.129.1 Altman's “gentle” singularity

Substack ↗

When Sam Altman publishes something on his blog, it's worth a read.

In The Gentle Singularity he argues that we've passed the event horizon of artificial intelligence. But so “gently” that we didn't even notice.

We already live with incredible digital intelligence but, after the initial shock, we got used to it fast. We went from marvelling that AI could write a paragraph to wondering when it will write whole books; from being surprised by medical diagnoses to wondering when it will develop the next medicine.

A subsistence farmer from the year 1000 would say we have fake jobs, and that capitalism is just a game to keep us entertained, since we have food and luxuries galore.

ChatGPT (150 times cheaper and greener in a single year) is an example of this acceleration. But the next technological leap will be in the real world.

🥧ff.129.2 Pi, FigureAI and 1 hour of “logistics”?

Substack ↗

LLMs aren't just democratising software. They're the simplest interface we've ever had for the physical world - for “talking to robots”.

So programming robots is now cheaper and more generalisable. That's why various “robot foundation models” are spreading:

π0, by Physical Intelligence, handles 8 architectures and a range of actions, like making coffee or opening popcorn.

Helix, by FigureAI, combines a “slow, semantic” brain (System 2) that understands complex sentences and new scenes with a “fast, motor” one (System 1) that acts.

Google's SAS Prompt translates the robot's x, y, z coordinates into movements described in words.
