🥤 ff.130.1 Plastic is fantastic

Substack ↗

Let's not demonise plastic: it does good too, especially for preserving food (Bressanini, always on the ball).

dario.bressanini A post shared by @dario.bressanini

As Italians, we should even be a little proud of it. See Natta's Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1963.

But, but, but. Its advantages, like flexibility and chemical durability, are also what make it hard to separate and recycle. Working with DOW, Google X proposes an AI that sorts waste by type of plastic.

Mind you, we can potentially turn plastic bottles into diamonds (Science 2022).

While we wait for that to be economically viable, what do we do?

💀 ff.130.4 The danse macabre

Microplastics are so widespread that not even superman-wannabe Bryan Johnson can escape them (💊 ff.49.4 The superman who takes 100 pills a day).

🩸 ff.130.5 Microplastics harden arteries

Substack ↗

But what effects do microplastics have on our health?

One is hardening the arteries, increasing inflammation and cardiac events, as the ever-reliable Eric Topol points out.

In particular, DEHP (a phthalate found in PVC) is estimated to have caused 356,000 additional deaths from cardiovascular disease, i.e. 13.5% of total global deaths (peaking at 16.8% in South Asia, where DEHP is most widespread).

So it's a good thing that the work Mega Mat, made with 500 recycled plastic mats, was shown in Bangkok.
