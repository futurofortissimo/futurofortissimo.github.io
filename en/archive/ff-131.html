📚 ff.131.1 One read

Substack ↗

Thinking about the most meaningful book of this year, I can't help going back to Change Your Thoughts – Change Your Life (☯️ ff.108.3 Be water).

It collects and interprets Lao Tzu's 81 verses, applying them to our tormented, digital, hyper-connected, optimising days.

For example, I reread part of verse 23. Interesting.

To talk little is natural: fierce winds don't blow all morning; a downpour doesn't last all day. Who makes them? Heaven and earth. But these are violent weather events, which is why they can't last long. If heaven and earth can't sustain violent action for long, how could people? Lao Tzu, Tao Te Ching

✒️ ff.131.4 Four reflections

Substack ↗

Ideas and quotes to mull over by the sea, waiting for Piotta's big wave.

Unlike material goods, which seem exciting at first but quickly lose their value, experiences gain value over time: they pay what I call “memory dividends”. Bill Perkins, Die with Zero (🎼 ff.111 Die with zero)

On the theme of the self and excessive self-awareness, optimisation, individualism (🎁 ff.68.4 Less ego and control, more flow and presence: Naval's cure) I found another gem:

The ego, the false self, [is] the root of all the evil that penetrates the earth and destroys human life, and with it, of course, the reality of time, the reality of lived presence. The ego lives only in the future and the past; it has no present moment; it is always in a hurry or dreaming. The root of our modern problem with time is neither technological, nor sociological, nor economic, nor psychological. It is metaphysical. It is a question of the very meaning of human life. Jacob Needleman, Time and Soul

The cyclical nature of moments. Beautiful, ugly, fleeting, always ephemeral; in the genius of the professor-turned-raver Cosmo.

I'd like to stop this train right now I wish I knew how I wish I knew how I wish it all were a little slower I wish I knew how To stretch out time I'd like to burn up and be born again I'd like to shield you from this wind And I'll enjoy every single moment Then let it go And then start over Cosmo, Momenti

Growing up means preferring vegetables to biscuits.

Happiness is like a cookie. When you are a child, you think cookies are very important and you want to have all the cookies in the world. You can't wait to be an adult, because then you can have as many cookies as you want, right? Yes, but as an adult you realise the cookie is a tool. A tool to make you eat your vegetables. And once you understand that, you eat your vegetables anyway and mostly stop eating cookies, because otherwise you risk getting diabetes and not being there for your kids. Joscha Bach, Lex Fridman Podcast #101
