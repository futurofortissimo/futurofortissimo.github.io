🎯 ff.132.1 An attention crisis?

Substack ↗

As a gamer and (ex) YouTuber with 50,000 subscribers, I care about video games.

Often demonised alongside 15-second TikToks, they're accused of destroying our attention.

But, but, but. Are we sure we can't concentrate any more? According to historian Daniel Immerwahr, that's not the case.

A couple of reflections he shared in a recent podcast with Adam Grant:

In the nineteenth century people said books were distracting. Today they're a symbol of concentration. Video games, like social feeds, generate flow, not distraction. And we lose hours to them, because they're so immersive.

The real question isn't “how much” we concentrate, but on what.

🕹️ ff.132.2 The benefits of video games

Substack ↗

And anyway, is time spent “playing on the PlayStation” really wasted?

Shooting in GTA helps you not do it in real life (🎮 ff.8.5 Killing boredom (and not human beings)). The link between violence and video games? Probably hot air.

Video games foster decision-making under stress, hand-eye coordination and teamwork. No wonder many founders have a gamer past: Kennan Davison, Top 200 in League of Legends, now CEO of Skio (YC S20).

Ash Brandin (The Gamer Educator on Instagram) also points out that some video games, like Braid, Edith Finch and Untitled Goose Game, foster emotional learning.

📐 ff.132.3 Measuring and modelling the world

Substack ↗

To “illude” (in-ludere, a word tied to the Latin ludus, play), a video game has to simulate, to model the world coherently (🎼 ff.98 Games and simulations).

Building virtual worlds also helps us understand the real one.

No wonder Demis Hassabis (DeepMind) - a former gamer himself - now “plays” at modelling cells (after the Nobel he received for modelling proteins with AI).

And the analogy is even closer: Genie 3 creates, in real time and even from a single image, interactive video games that stay consistent over time.

QED. SimCity was born when Will Wright realised that building the world of his arcade shooter was more fun than playing it.
