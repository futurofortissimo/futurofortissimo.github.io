💲 ff.135.1 The costs of Artificial Intelligence

Substack ↗

According to some, ChatGPT is pushing up electricity bills: Federal Reserve data show an increase from 0.17 to 0.19 $/kWh (in Europe, eclipsed by the far more tangible effects of the war in Ukraine).

But, but, but. It isn't an AI problem, it's energy short-sightedness: Germany and the United Kingdom produce less electricity than 40 years ago…

Nerd corner. AI's energy use (Nature 2025):

0.5 Wh = energy for one image 1000 Wh/20(h)/60(min) = 0.83 Wh energy for one minute of laptop use

🌊 ff.135.2 A (rogue) energy wave

Substack ↗

All this is nothing compared with the plans for the future:

Musk wants to buy 50 million H100s in the next 5 years (soaking up 35 GW, 2% of today's global electricity consumption).

Altman wants to remove the limits imposed by energy production:

With 10 gigawatts of compute, AI can figure out how to cure cancer. Or with 10 gigawatts of compute, AI can figure out how to provide customized tutoring to every student on earth. If we are limited by compute, we'll have to choose which one to prioritize. Our vision is simple: we want to create a factory that can produce a gigawatt of new AI infrastructure every week. Sam Altman, Abundant Intelligence

Fortunately, one solution is solar: in just 10 years from 0 to 2073 TWh (237 GW of instantaneous power, total energy produced divided by the hours in a year).

Now you know. Investment in solar ($500B) has overtaken investment in aviation during the Second World War ($400B, adjusted for inflation).

♾️ ff.135.3 Are we in the singularity?

Substack ↗

All this energy, though, is for (and has already been used for) something.

For instance, to “solve” mathematics.

GPT-5 Pro answers “FrontierMath Tier 4” problems (so hard that, assuming they get solved at all, they take expert mathematicians weeks of work).

This breakthrough is hugely important: physics, chemistry and - in the end - biology all depend, in cascade, on mathematics.

It's so astonishing that Alex Wissner, a real whizz-kid, says we're in the singularity, describing it as an optical illusion that from afar looks like a “vertical asymptote”, but that “when you're in the middle of it... feels fairly continuous”.

☕ ff.135.4 AGI advances: the Chinese “coffee”

Substack ↗

AGI and the singularity are a bonus: Dave Blundin, in the same podcast, points out that even if AI development stopped today, it would take years to digest, explore and implement its potential applications.

Current models are already smart enough, but they need to be deployed in robots, factories and logistics, while cutting their energy use.

And so China is back - rampant: because of the embargo on the most powerful GPUs (🎼 ff.82 A war fought with chips) it has been forced to squeeze the “coffee beans” (= bits) with less powerful “coffee machines” (= GPUs) (an analogy by Ryan Cunningham).

No wonder, then, that at the recent AI Conference in Shanghai, Professor Wang Yu coined “watt-to-bit”, a metric for ranking the efficiency of AI models (Energy-Compute Theory).

No wonder, again, that the ARC Prize leaderboard includes cost per task (which traces a Pareto frontier currently occupied by OpenAI's o3 models).
