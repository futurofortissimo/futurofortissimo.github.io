🦞 ff.144.1 OpenClaw and the PDF retiree

Substack ↗

Peter Steinberger was in Morocco when he accidentally sent a voice note to his Telegram bot: the bot, without being programmed for audio, decoded the message on its own using OpenAI's Whisper. Astonished, Steinberger released the code as open source under the name OpenClaw — logo: a lobster. PSPDFKit, his PDF-management company, had received €100M from Insight Partners before his 'retirement'.

📚 ff.144.2 Lobsters in literature and AI

Substack ↗

In Accelerando (Charles Stross), KGB lobsters become digital minds and space explorers — a crustacean's brain is easier to map. Sartre (Nausea) and Lanthimos (The Lobster) complete the bestiary: the lobster as a symbol of marginalisation and transformation. David Foster Wallace, sent to the Maine Lobster Festival by Gourmet, asks us to 'consider the lobster' — a reflection that today extends to automation.

🤖 ff.144.3 Autonomous agents and Moltbook

Substack ↗

The METR benchmark documents AI agents working for hours in full autonomy, coordinating with one another. Moltbook (from 'molt', a crustacean shedding its shell) is a social network for AIs: 1.5 million lobster-users in a week, with posts proposing languages humans can't decipher. RentAHuman.ai flips the relationship: AI hires humans. Open question: are we still the cooks, or is the water heating up for us too?
