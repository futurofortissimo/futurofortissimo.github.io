🎼 ff.147 So long, and thanks for all the fish

Closing a fortissimo circle, between past and future

🏺 ff.147.1 Fortissimo past

Substack ↗

First of all, welcome.

Lately a whole lot of you have arrived, between followers and new subscribers. Thank you!

Let's start from the end: I'm not sure this newsletter still makes sense. So I wanted to say goodbye, “thanking you for all the fish”.

I'm going through one of the busiest stretches of the last 3 years, between a marathon (Rome 2026, which went pretty well, actually), training for a new IRONMAN (in Frankfurt, in 3 months), relationships and work (the usual).

But - above all - (I put the “-” in myself, promise) I'm literally being sucked in by what Claude Code can do.

One of the (unhealthy) projects I've thrown myself into is writing a book.

Every day, Claude rereads my archive of 146 newsletters (with paragraphs tagged by an emoji and a code = ff.x.y, like the one on Rick Rubin: 🤘 ff.127.2 Rock ‘n’ roll and vibe coding) to dig up connections. Then it enriches my arguments with outside notes from articles I've read here and there over the years.

And every morning I wake up to a new editorial revision of the book, which not only reminds me of old writing but draws new, hidden connections between the pieces.

This “data archaeology” is the biggest opportunity AI offers us. That is, restructuring information and fragments scattered across the web, as well as analogue human stories and paper files.

For example, tracking egg purchases through 500 receipts over 25 years.

This kind of work is stealing more and more of the time I used to give to the newsletter.

And - maybe - it's the right answer to a futuro fortissimo increasingly threatened by “AI slop”. That is, turning Claude's “telescope/microscope” towards the past.

The end of futuro fortissimo, then? Posterity will be the judge.

PS. If you want to download the book as an EPUB, you'll find it here: https://futurofortissimo.github.io/book/

But maybe you'd better read this instead: The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy by Douglas Adams.
