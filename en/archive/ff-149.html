3️⃣ ff.149.1 Give me three words…

Substack ↗

Futuro Fortissimo's first issue on climate and renewables went out on a cold March day four years ago ( 🎼 ff.12 Sun, heart and love ). Cold March, mind you, in Bergamo. In the south-western United States this year, a heatwave pushed temperatures to mid-June levels. In short, a sun (heart and love) that keeps warming our planet and our oceans more and more, partly because emissions are falling (of sulphites, to be precise 🕳️ ff.56.2 We are already geoengineers ). Stopping at what we “see” today is short-sighted (yes, that's meant to be funny): the future is a projection of today, not an interpolation of yesterday. In this issue I'll try to shed (solar) light on the latest developments that give us hope for a different future. A better one. But first…

🧮 ff.149.2 A matter of hours…

Substack ↗

… a quick energy guide, to put numbers and labels on things (after all, 🎼 ff.32 Numbers don't lie ). Un'ora sola ti vorrei — just one hour, as the old Italian song goes. First, a reminder of the units for power (W, often GW) and for energy produced/consumed (often TWh). How they relate: 1 GW “producing” for a year (8,760 hours) = 8.76 TWh. And, putting energy in perspective: 1 GW = 450,000 Italian households 510 GW (added in 2025) = electricity output of India + Brazil 2,392 GW = total solar = 10% of the world's electricity Electricity = 20% of the world's energy

🃏 ff.149.3 Texas hold 'em

Substack ↗

With these “rulers” in hand, let's measure the latest developments. In Texas - forever the symbol of American oil - 2025 saw solar output rise by 40%, peaking at 7 TWh in August (~1 GW of energy produced, i.e. our famous 450,000 Italian households). Yes, the sun doesn't shine at night. So add 15 GW from wind too. So much so that in March 2026 renewables covered 70% of Texas's electricity . PS. As with many innovative technologies (batteries, solar panels, wind), China rules the roost: the top Chinese companies added +125 GW of wind (~25 GW outside China). Not just solar: wind is no joke either (via futuro fortissimo) And - where there's no shortage of sun, in Africa - an “Arab” revolution of sorts is under way too: Nigeria, Algeria, Congo and Mozambique lead the pack (1.5-0.4 GW of solar panels imported from China).

🤖 ff.149.4 Going to the Maximo

Substack ↗

Why the acceleration? By now, generating renewable electricity simply makes technological sense. One bottleneck is installation. But here another trend dear to futuro fortissimo comes to the rescue: robotics. Maximo (developed by AES, a company backed by NVIDIA) has just finished installing 100 MW of solar panels in California (the equivalent of 0.5% of Texas's output, installed in 7 months ). Maximo installs one panel a minute. So, every month, 2,500 Italian households: 480 panels a day (60 min x 8h) x 20 working days = 9,600 panels x output of one panel = 500 W = 4.8 MW

🗽 ff.149.5 Freedom (from gas and oil)

Substack ↗

Solar is also a chance to cut our dependence on gas (Russia) or oil (Iran). A dependence that - in times of war in Ukraine or with the Strait of Hormuz closed - hurts plenty of countries, Norway excepted, of course… From Prof G Media Egypt, for example, has seen its energy costs double in two months and could only be rescued by Chinese wind power . But China itself, to cut its reliance on imports, is considering blocking solar exports . So, who will win? Maximo or Trump? Posterity will be the judge.
