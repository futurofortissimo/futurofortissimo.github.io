🎼 ff.156 Aulin?

What do you do when your head (and your pumpkin) explodes, every single day?

👨‍🌾 ff.156.1 From riches to rags?

Substack ↗

I haven't written, haven't pressed a keyboard, in far too long. I missed it. Typing feels more and more like a new endurance sport: a physiological response to texts generated with a click, which are never, though, exactly what you had in mind. Thoughts are now like steps: to be counted with a smartwatch, every day. Thinking looks more and more like moving: forcing yourself to write or to run is the same thing. Speaking of moving, of sport: it's been an intense few months. After the IRONMAN failure (cancelled because of climate change and Frankfurt's 37° in June), I had to recharge my batteries. Motivation after the double failure (at Cervia 2025 I'd got a puncture on the bike…) was at an all-time low. Or rather, the mental energy for any other project was. Besides, the fact that two years of work had come to nothing made me reconsider a lot of routines. Among them, writing Futuro Fortissimo. And not just that: there was yet another agentic explosion. As with every significant leap in AI, I wanted to explore the new possibilities. For two months I threw myself into a project, Mercatorum, even thinking of building a startup. NOTE. Mercatorum starts from an observation: we make lots of half-empty trips, say between home, work and a hobby. We could use them to bring the quality of local producers (who don't have Amazon-style logistics) to colleagues, five-a-side football friends or fellow villagers (members, in Mercatorum, of “Circles”). You can try it (and let me know what you'd improve): https://app.via-mercatorum.com/ Maybe one day it'll take off, becoming a sort of “BlaBlaCar for products”. Meanwhile, I carry on with other distractions.

🧞‍♂️ ff.156.2 The Genie out of the lamp

Substack ↗

It isn't easy to understand who you want to be, to hold on to values and long-term projects, when every day the world seems different from yesterday's. Ever more simulated, perhaps, by Genie. We're all going through it. As Nathaniel Drew says in his latest video: Over the last twelve months I've spent an enormous amount of time reading, listening to and experimenting with artificial intelligence tools. Really an enormous amount of time. And all of this stirs up an incredibly wide range of emotions in me. Sometimes fear, sometimes wonder, other times joy and excitement about the possibilities opening up. In short, I feel very different emotions. And worry often wins. I frequently find myself in that state of mind. I think it partly depends on how my mind reacts to what's happening, but I also think it's something I'm seeing in many other people. There's a lot of worry around me. And when it isn't artificial intelligence, with its countless applications and the consequences it could have across different sectors, then it's war, climate change or some other development that breeds unease. What I find truly fascinating, though, is that not everyone reacts the same way. In fact, that's one of the most beautiful things I'm noticing: people can react in profoundly different ways, reach completely different conclusions and, really, there is no single right reaction. … speaking of eternal returns, btw, we met Nathaniel a few centuries ago, in 🍄 ff.2.6 Psychedelia, take me away (part 2)

♾️ ff.156.3 The eternal return

Substack ↗

Anyway, between Claude 5.5 and GPT Sol-Astra-Terra 6.1, between yet another marathon and the next, between yet another app and the next, I received a message. And I understood that perhaps what I've done over the last two years, the last five years, makes sense. And that it makes sense to keep doing it. This message from Giorgio, more than the amount of the PayPal donation that came with it, reminded me of something rather simple. That perhaps what I've done over the last two years, the last five years, makes sense. Even without a finish line. Even without a startup. Even without knowing exactly where it will lead. I reach this thought while looking out of the window of a Starbucks that smells of pumpkin spice latte: a florist is arranging the last pumpkins, ready to be carved tomorrow, the “holiday”, another Saturday in the Village. And yet this Aulin tastes of something different. In the singularity, every year feels like another life and even the cycle of the seasons seems to fade. Maybe that's why I've come back, like an oyster, to cling to this QWERTY: to find the flow again, to feel a little lactic acid, but in the brain, to feel a little human in front of a future that is perhaps too fortissimo.
