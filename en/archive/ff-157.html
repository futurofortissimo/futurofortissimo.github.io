🧠 ff.157.1 The world's smallest tasting menu

Substack ↗

Distracted first by the social-media mix, then by AI, in this endless hyper-stimulation the only way to pay attention to things is to force ourselves to spend hundreds of euros. For a Michelin dinner, say. Or not? Maybe not. I'm reminded of that time when, during a yoga session with a nutritionist, we started savouring a walnut, asked to look carefully, for several minutes, at its ridges. And so my brain stopped to look at another “brain” (and took a flight of fancy towards one of the favourite books of my teens: The Universe in a Nutshell. Attention is back, then, in a thread that has been running for a long time ( ⚠️ ff.83.2 Pay attention! ). Let's see in which other field it might resurface.

🍽️ ff.157.2 From Michelin stars to GLP-1

Substack ↗

Food is back too, and maybe that's no accident: by now eating is one of the few “real” activities we still do. And amid all the visual and sonic overstimulation, only taste (for now) stays a little safer. I don't know about you, but for me a meal has become an almost religious moment. A moment to switch off every other stimulus. To stop and catch my breath a little. A bulwark of physiology. Bonus. Since we're talking about the tangle of digital (and AI) and physical, a reminder that what's on the plate (but not around the waist): GLP-1 brings in more revenue than AI.

🤹‍♀️ ff.157.3 Juggling 3 jobs?

Substack ↗

We were already multitasking. Now, with agents that in a few lines can turn, for example, the Orobie Alps into Minecraft, we can have endless jobs too. Or at least 3, according to ilovecreatives. During an episode of Nicer Tuesdays in Los Angeles, Jennifer Puno suggests juggling between jobs that pay and jobs you're passionate about. Moving beyond ikigai ( 🗾 ff.116.4 I'm Japanese! ): the part of the talk I liked most is the shift from the question “What is my passion?” to “What is my community missing? Where can I be useful?”: that's how she found meaning in her life, in giving and creating what helps others ( 🔺ff.62.4 The pyramid inside all of us ). And she leaves you, and I leave you, with an invitation: learn to live with the destabilising feeling of being one, no one and one hundred thousand. To live with your “slashiness”. Signed, Michele (engineer/writer/gamer/athlete/coder/…). Until the next 10th, 20th or 30th!
