🔢 ff.158.1 Prime numbers and how to hunt them

Substack ↗

Yes, I know. It isn't the 20th. And Futuro Fortissimo shouldn't be landing in your inbox today. It's the 21st. And 21 isn't even prime, if you want to be picky. But it's 3 times 7. And those two definitely are. Years ago, when I set off on the “path of science”, my cousin - an engineer too - gave me this book: I never imagined I'd post it in a newsletter, in a parallel world, years later. Why are we talking about The Music of the Primes? Because yesterday, the 20th, the day after Anthropic's stock-market listing and the release of GTA, a Turkish researcher at that very company (no, not Rockstar…), Levent Alpöge, published a four-page note on his website (after asking Amodei for permission). Apparently, to report that he had solved the Riemann Hypothesis: I believe I have discovered, working with Claude, a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. […] what I saw along the way amazed me so much that I believe it is the dawn, across all of science, of something much bigger. Formulated 150 years ago, it describes a function that captures the apparent “chaos” in the distribution of prime numbers. It was solved with the equivalent of 250,000 pages of code (17 million lines of text…). The most disorienting part? Alpöge admits he hasn't yet mastered what he has just published: he writes that Claude “flew, danced and sparkled near the finish line”, and that he will need time to understand the proof. That's not a problem. Just as it isn't a problem that we don't understand a plane or a crane, able to reach 900 km/h or lift tonnes. But it's normal if your stomach tightens a little: it still happens to me too, sometimes, boarding a plane or walking under a suspended load.

🔭 ff.158.2 “Conceptual” microscopes

Substack ↗

Anyway, we've been using technology to expand our consciousness for centuries. ChatGPT is nothing particularly new in that sense: we are more aware thanks to microscopes, ultrasound sensors, GPS, telescopes…). We're just discovering a new “sensor”, but for words. What's astonishing, if anything, is that words themselves can “magnify ideas” ( 🧠 ff.83.3 Language and thought ).

😮 ff.158.3 Terrifying awe?

Substack ↗

I know, all this is enigmatic. Frightening. Better: it's all awe, a disorienting “wow”. Awe has no exact equivalent in Italian (and I thought I'd already written about it, since I particularly loved Keltner's book). But given that gap, let's try to understand it: what does it mean to be “in awe”? It's the feeling of inexplicable wonder that bursts out in front of a starry sky, a huge cathedral, but also at the deathbed of a loved one. Perceiving something that exceeds our understanding. Today, a new item joins that list: getting an answer from Claude and the like. Solving Riemann is the first awe that hits the brain directly.

❌ ff.158.4 Everyone gets it wrong

Substack ↗

This anxious wonder grows when you consider that nobody - not even the smartest people - managed to grasp the speed of all this: 2023: Stephen Wolfram (the Wolfram Alpha guy) complaining about ⚠️ ff.48.4 ChatGPT's (mathematical) limits, 2026, March: Terence Tao (Fields Medal 2006…) defending mathematical prizes because, he argued, “for problems like the Riemann hypothesis we're fairly sure a new kind of mathematics needs to be created” (confirming his view just over a month ago, on Numberphile). Then again, not even Galileo or Thomson (telescope, electron) could have foreseen galaxies and positrons (let alone the technologies that followed, like space stations and positron emission tomography). I have no more words. Or numbers. I just want to invite you to admire, in silence, unconditionally welcoming all the infinity contained in this moment. Until the next 10th, 20th or 30th (or 31st…)!
