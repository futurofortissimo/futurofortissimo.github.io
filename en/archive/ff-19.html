💦 ff.19.1 Polluting flatulence

Substack ↗

By now it's common knowledge that cows aren't exactly eco-friendly. After China, with their ruminant stomachs and the methane they produce, they contribute an amount of CO2 equivalent higher than the United States. These emissions are also very similar to those due to transport (planes, trains, cars) worldwide.

TDLR: 🐄 = 🇺🇸 = 🚄 + 🚌 + 🚑 + ✈️

Ourworldindata breaks down food pollution into its various factors, such as land use, methane production, farming emissions, and the pollution tied to animal feed, processing and transport of the finished product.

Below, the emissions (in kg CO2eq per kg of product) of the most common foods:

Cows, if also used for milk and dairy, pollute 1/3 as much as their "beef" counterparts. Plant-based foods are on average 10 times less polluting than their animal counterparts. Hazelnuts make a positive contribution, because their low land-use impact offsets the other emissions. Milk pollutes 3 times its weight, soy milk 1/3 of regular milk. Coffee and chocolate are fairly polluting, although we consume them in smaller amounts (roughly, one chocolate bar = 0.5kg of rice = 30 bananas).

🍔 ff.19.2 Hemingway's favourite hamburger

Substack ↗

Hemingway's father raised his son with one rule: "eat everything you kill". Hence his fortissimo attraction to hunting and fishing, but also his respect for animals, never preyed upon without - at the very least - the aim of eating them (porcupines and lions included). This respect also shines through in his novella The Old Man and the Sea.

Hemingway was a passionate eater and drinker. So much so that in the society notes from his home in Cuba (which lasted 20 years) we find the recipe for his favourite hamburger:

Ground beef, onions, garlic, India relish, and capers, cooked so the edges were crispy but the center red and juicy

Served in a bun, with nothing else

In short, a simple hamburger, well done on the edges and soft in the middle. The Indian spices rule. If you want to recreate it, you'll find the details here

♨️ ff.19.3 Killer stoves

Substack ↗

A Stanford study found that gas stoves release a significant amount of methane, even when switched off (about 1% of what they burn).

By the numbers: the polluting effect is relatively small, since the emissions from the leaks equal the use of 0.5 million cars (out of 276 million in the United States, and about 1.4 billion motor vehicles worldwide). So we're talking about 0.2% of the pollution from US cars.

Still, molecules are molecules.

🐖 ff.19.4 Cool sustainable choices

Substack ↗

The Swedish agency Everland redesigned the packaging of the products of the French green startup La Vie. The goal? To break away from the classic packaging of the "organic", "sustainable" and vegan world, in order to grow its customer base.

Using mainly pink, which recalls bacon (paired with a very vegetarian green), and wrapping the packaging in ironic and peaceful themes, Everland tries to make La Vie products more appealing (marketing-wise), maybe even cooling down the feuds between vegans and everyone else.

💉 ff.19.5 3D-printed meat

Substack ↗

To make meat more sustainable, while keeping a texture and flavour as close as possible to the animal version, Redefine Meat produces its vegan burgers with a 3D printer.

Texture matters. Animal meat is made of muscle fibres whose arrangement varies depending on the cut. This leads to different cooking, consistency and flavour. Managing to replicate the marbling with 3D printing is another step towards a tasty, sustainable lunch.

Who knows what Hemingway would think of it.
