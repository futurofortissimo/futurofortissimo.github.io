🐖 ff.25.1 Pig heart transplants

Substack ↗

Le creature difuori guardavano dal maiale all'uomo e dall'uomo al maiale e ancora dal maiale all'uomo, ma già era loro impossibile distinguerefra i due. George Orwell,La Fattoria degli Animali

Did you hear about the first pig-to-human heart transplant with no rejection? To make it happen, they had to switch off a gene found in many animals, pigs included, but not in monkeys or humans. In fact, gal-transferase creates a layer of an antigen that our immune system rejects, causing rejection.

Hogwash? The recipient of this donation died, probably from a bovine infection (controllable, but it got out of hand in this case).

Clearly we're only at the beginning and there are critical issues. But transplants may be a solution. To what, you ask?

🫀 ff.25.2 The scale of the transplant problem

Substack ↗

The main problem is that the number of transplant requests exceeds the available organs, which are in short, fortissimo short, supply.

The Financial Times has a great report on this. In the United States alone, about 60,000 people are waiting for a transplant. Only 65% of them get one.

Which organs are most in demand? Kidneys (83%), liver (10%) and heart (3%).

Oh, and hair too, obviously. The company dNovo is working on reprogramming pluripotent cells to restore the growth of those little strands.

🙊 ff.25.3 Translating pig language

Substack ↗

Being able to communicate with (or at least interpret) the signals coming from other animals could matter in many ways: ensuring their well-being, understanding how much they suffer, or holding interesting philosophical discussions.

Recently, artificial intelligence was used to sample and interpret pig grunts, recorded in situations of emotional stress or well-being. Once trained to recognise the patterns, the system was able to match the animals' sounds with their mood with 90% accuracy.

📦 ff.25.4 Amazon for pets: a bubble?

Substack ↗

Ryan Cohen is a very peculiar character. He reminds me a bit of Elon Musk, but at times he's even more of a living meme. Why are we talking about him? Because he made a fortune with animals.

ff is about this: trends, and how to follow them (or get ahead of them?).

Startup Stories@StartupStoriezRyan Cohen is an entrepreneur, meme-god, and chairman of wallstreetbets darling: $GME.

But long before the short squeezes and 🚀🚀 of 2021, Cohen set out to revolutionize the pet market with@Chewy🐶

The story of@ryancohen’s underdog (get it?) effort to take on Amazon 🧵9:00 PM ∙ Dec 21, 202140Likes8Retweets

Founder of Chewy, the Amazon of pets, he's now an executive at GameStop. A perfectly logical career move, since nobody lives a dog's life more than gamers.

Ok, that wasn't funny.

It's interesting how interest in pets also exploded during the loneliness of the pandemic.

But, but, but… Just as Zoom and Peloton "burned" all the value they'd accumulated over the last two years, Chewy's value followed the same trend (down 50% since the start of the year).

This leads to an interesting thought: are the digitalisation of shopping, the hours in front of the computer, the metaverse and cryptocurrencies just the result of a huge pandemic boredom, or did the future speed up thanks to COVID-19?

For sure, by "riding" the pandemic wave, Cohen made big money. Maybe, though, rather than following trends, you need to predict them and get out once they're a given. Inflated.

With ff we try to trace possible futures before they become visible to most people.
