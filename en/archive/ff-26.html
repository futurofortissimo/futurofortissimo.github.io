👧 ff.26.1 Genuine (virtual) influencers

Substack ↗

Apparently, there are fully digital influencers. That alone is a trend nobody saw coming. To fight the perfection of social media (which is limitless when control over the character is total, given its digital nature), Jesse Zhang created Angie, a virtual influencer with imperfect skin who often cries, embodying the insecurities typical of her age.

0️⃣ ff.26.2 (Almost) zero-impact websites

Substack ↗

Even if the endless scroll of social media feels free, besides paying for it with our data, it also has an impact on energy emissions. To remind us of this, an Amsterdam design studio redesigned the Volkswagen website to make it more sustainable.

By removing colours, high-definition images and animations, they got down to 0.02 grams of CO2 emitted per page view, about 100 times less than average.

100 YouTube pages = two plates of rice. YouTube's emissions per page view, calculated by websitecarbon.com, are 1/100 of the emissions needed to produce 150g of rice.

🌾 ff.26.3 The superfoods that will save the planet

Substack ↗

Rice, by the way, is eco-friendly. Wheat, which yields only once a year and constantly needs replanting, slightly less so. A recently proposed alternative is Kernza (Thinopyrum intermedium). Unlike wheat, this special-K is perennial and produces seeds cyclically.

Patagonia (which apparently also makes beer) used it for an alternative Pale Ale.
