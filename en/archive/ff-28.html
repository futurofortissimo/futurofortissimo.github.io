☢️ ff.28.1 Artificial intelligence for nuclear fusion

Substack ↗

Nuclear fusion has been predicted for decades, as we discussed in 🎼 ff.12 Sun, heart and love.

This time it's different! (?) A few months ago Nature published the use of neural networks to control the position and shape of the plasma in a nuclear fusion reactor. In practice, control of the plant's operation (with magnetic actuators) was handed to a Deep Reinforcement Learning system. Replacing complex physics calculations that have to happen extremely fast, AI can “learn” to manage the system beforehand and be much faster in execution.

A plasma snowflake. With this system they obtained various types of plasma, including non-spherical and less conventional shapes (a snowflake). Exploring its geometric properties is one of the fundamental steps towards finding the fusion process able to generate more energy than the system uses.

In rich words:

We successfully produce and control a diverse set ofplasma configurations on the Tokamak à Configuration Variable1,2, including elongated, conventional shapes, as well as advanced configurations, such as negative triangularity and ‘snowflake’ configurations […]. Tokamak magnetic control is one ofthe most complex real-world systems to which RL has been applied. This is a promising new directionfor plasma controller design, with the potential to acceleratefusion science, explore new configurations and aid infuture tokamak development.

🧆 ff.28.2 Finding the bean in the jar

Substack ↗

X, the moonshot factory, is a Google-linked company that does cool stuff.

Project Mineral is one of the most interesting projects: it uses a mix of robotics, image recognition and biology to improve agriculture, monitor harvests and map new crop varieties to boost biodiversity and nutrition.

The grass is always greener. Of the 30,000 known edible plant species, only 1% is cultivated by humans. We don't know what nutritional value 99% of known plants could offer (because the analyses are missing). Project Mineral aims to close this knowledge gap.

🗺️ ff.28.3 Google Maps cuts emissions

Substack ↗

Google has implemented an eco-friendly routing system on Maps. By avoiding traffic jams, and therefore more time with the engine running, it already helps cut emissions. It has also recently added options for bikes and e-scooters and started integrating charging stations for electric vehicles into routes, encouraging their use and reducing “dead battery” anxiety. Google estimates these features could avoid 1M tonnes of CO2 emissions (the emissions from using 200,000 cars) every year.

🩺 ff.28.4 Wear your vest (or are you feeling lucky?)

Substack ↗

Google has launched a data management platform called Care Studio, which presents the doctors of the future with a given patient's health data, ensuring simple, AI-assisted access. There's a short video showing how it works.

Nest Hub, not just a voice assistant. Speaking of wellbeing, Nest Hub 2, Google's mini-tablet for the smart home, can monitor the sleep-wake cycle with 3D radar and help disabled people with communication difficulties express themselves or make themselves understood.
