👽 ff.3.2 What are you really, Meta? (part 2)

Substack ↗

In the chat below, Mark Zuckerberg talks about the process that led him to Meta and what he'll try to integrate.

Gary (1.10): What led you to Meta? Mark (2.46): Meta is a way of saying that we don't just work on social media, but on technologies [like Oculus, acquired 7 years ago, ed.] to create contacts, groups, relationships. And to improve them. Gary (4.30): Oculus! You've been working on it for 7 years. Feels like yesterday. But at the same time it took a long time to digest that acquisition. Mark (6.40): Digital communication started with SMS, then images (after cameras were built into phones), now it goes through video. But that's not the end point. Holograms could be the next step within five years. Gary (7.20): Do you think it could happen in just 3-5 years? I saw you fencing against the world champion, projected Obi-Wan Kenobi style. Mark (8.40). Augmented reality is different from virtual reality. Virtual reality is already here, for example with Oculus Quest. But augmented reality needs a lot of engineering and work on size. Gary (10:00): Can we shrink headsets down to a contact lens? Mark (10:30): I don't know, you still need to power it and connect it to the internet. For now, the technology isn't there. Gary (11:00): 5 years ago there was PokemonGo. Since then, though, there haven't been other large-scale examples of augmented reality. What do you think? Mark (11:12): PokemonGo is more about location; I don't think it's really augmented reality. Instagram filters are more of an example in this direction. A necessary aspect on the way to augmented reality is touch: augmented reality is, for example, feeling a friend's digital "high five". We're investing 10 billion a year in these devices.

Gary (17:00): Can you give me a sense of the WhatsApp and Instagram acquisitions? Mark (11:12): It's amazing to see how ways of communicating have evolved. We were the first to create the newsfeed. But honestly, we didn't develop every possible way of communicating. Hence the acquisitions. Gary (19:13): The newsfeed! When it was introduced and you could no longer just log into Facebook and post on your contacts' "wall", loads of people protested. But now every social network is built around the newsfeed. And what about web3?

Mark (24:00): Web3 and NFTs (non-fungible tokens) are an important element of the metaverse. Each user will be a unique reality, with their own digital objects (tokens) and cross-platform connections, not compartmentalised in isolated apps as happens now. To be continued…

👾 ff.3.5 Discord

Substack ↗

What Discord is: Discord was born as a calling server to guarantee clean conversations, without lag or robotic voices. Adopted first in the gaming world, today it has a range of uses, with servers devoted to finance, learning and cryptocurrencies.

What makes it different: the ability to share music and videos, to watch a film together. Also, a bit like Clubhouse, some Discord channels are invite-only. That's why many NFT-related groups (see ff.3.7 for details) are building their identity around this technology: they create digital lounges accessible only with certain carefully verified prerequisites.
