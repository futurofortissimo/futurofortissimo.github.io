👨‍🏫 ff.30.1 A short introduction

Substack ↗

Before diving into DALL-E 2's art, the explainer.

DALL-E is a name that blends the art of Salvador Dalí and Disney's cute little robot. It's an artificial intelligence developed by the OpenAI folks (the same ones who beat the world GO champion; we talked about it in 🎼 ff.18 Between digital art and AI) able to generate images consistent with a text input.

DALL-E can also place, in a given image, any kind of object we want, like a dog in a museum. And it does so by adapting the style to the location:

For now the model is accessible only to a few people, given the ethical issues that could arise from its misuse.

👂 ff.30.4 Understanding Picasso

Substack ↗

Another Twitter user suggested using the software's regression ability (when it's asked, for example, to show the dynamic steps from one image to another) to analyse Picasso's sketches.

Starting and ending on the artist's drawings, what's shown in between is a wandering / imagining of intermediate phases between the two forms. Thanks to DALL-E, then, we see the creative mental process that might have led Picasso from one idea to the next. The simplifications and “prototypes” between the various sketches.

Sarebbe molto interessante preservare, a livello visivo, non lefasi ma le metamorfosi di un immagine. In questo modo, potremmo scoprire il percorso seguito dal cervello per materializzare un sogno. Pablo Picasso,1935

📈 ff.30.5 The improvements in just one year

Substack ↗

What's phenomenal about artificial intelligence is the speed of its development. For years people have predicted that the physical (quantum) limits of Moore's Law would halt progress in computing power. The reality is that humans keep inventing loads of ways around them.

In one year, DALL-E (now version 2.0) improved its results, reaching 4 times the resolution. Seeing is believing.

“An avocado-shaped chair”.

DALL-E 1

DALL-E 2

The next step? Probably video creation. Imagine the power of a tool that can depict, for example, any film plot that comes to mind. And maybe do it in an afternoon.

The creator economy of the future will put today's influencers to shame.
