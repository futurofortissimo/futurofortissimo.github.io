🎼 ff.31 Summer is starting

How to spend August thinking about a fortissimo future

📚 ff.31.1 One read

Substack ↗

Catherine Price is an American journalist and writer. I discovered her recently in some podcast lost in the digital maze.

Detox time. Her book How to Break Up with Your Phone was a pleasant read. It offers food for thought on our relationship with phones, social media and the anxieties that connectivity, staying on top of everything and chasing likes create. It also proposes a 30-day guide to step back a bit from the loop of notifications and dopamine that is really leaving its mark on us psychologically.

LOOKUP. This app developed by a dear friend could help you in the detox phase.

📜 ff.31.2 Two articles or reports

Substack ↗

Then there are two little reads of reports or forecasts on the future (in climate and tech) that I'm keen to share.

Move over, Tolkien. The IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change) has written a climate report of 3000+ pages. But there's also a soft 40-page version that still sums up the key points.

The ARK investment fund (-53% YTD at the time of writing, 😭) has loads of great content on the future. I suggest their analysis of the main ideas for 2022, which include blockchain, electric and autonomous vehicles, genomics, artificial intelligence and robotics. And their positive mutual connection. You'll find everything at the following link: ARK 2022 Big Ideas

🎙️ ff.31.3 Three podcasts

Substack ↗

Reading is great, but listening even more so. Like, I think, many of you, podcasts are now part of my daily life (especially when I cycle to work, combining productive and healthy time).

Lex Fridman, an MIT researcher, half nerd and half romantic, has a series of really interesting (if longish) conversations. In the video above, he talks with Jonathan Haidt, social psychologist and professor of Ethical Leadership at New York University's Stern School of Business. The topic of his podcast appearance? Precisely the effect of smartphones/social media on our mental health. Especially on teenagers.

Ryan Holiday is the author of The Daily Stoic, a calendar-book with quotes from the greatest Stoics adapted to modern life. In this chat, he picks up many of the themes from his book.

If instead you want to understand how to make the most of your hours of sleep, there's Andrew Huberman.

✒️ ff.31.4 Four reflections on self-improvement and reaching big goals

Substack ↗

And now four somewhat scattered, somewhat motivational quotes.

Almost all good things take much longer than you think. Get busy and don't worry about how long it will take. James Clear

Presta attenzione a una giornata in cui arrivi estremamente soddisfatto. Non è un giorno in cui te ne sei stato beato nel relax; è un giorno in cui eri pieno di cose dafare e le hai portate a termine. Margaret Thatcher

Before trying to increase your willpower, try removing friction from the environment around you. James Clear

Ti sei mai accorto che a volte quando qualcosa t’importa di meno, ti riesce meglio? Hai mai notato che spesso è la persona meno coinvolta emotivamente nel successo di qualcosa chefinisce per ottenerlo? Che a volte quando smetti di sbatterti, tutto sembra andare a posto? Mark Manson,La sottile arte di fare quello che c***o ti pare

🤞 ff.31.5 Five tips for the summer

Substack ↗

Put on sunscreen and don't get burnt. Think about why you're touching your phone, every time you touch it; try to look the people you talk to in the eye more. At least once, say no to a burger. Get around more by bike than by car. Spend time in nature, in silence, listen to the rustle of the air.
