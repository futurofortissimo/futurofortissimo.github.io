🕛 ff.35.2 Smartwatches and more

Substack ↗

Spending in the wellness category obviously includes wearables, devices we carry with us all the time that monitor various aspects of our physiology. Here's the split between smartwatches, wireless earbuds, smart bands and “other”:

Other? What's in this category? Some notable examples:

The Oura Ring is a ring that measures HRV, heart rate variability (some smartwatches do it too, and it's not clear which is more accurate). HRV indicates how much fatigue you've built up. So it suggests to elite athletes the kind of training compatible with their recovery.

Supersapiens, meanwhile, offers a blood glucose monitor. This lets athletes “never run out of fuel” by suggesting when to eat. It can also be used to assess the glycaemic response to various foods.

Speaking of glycaemic responses: Zoe does exactly that. It sends a kit for various physiological measurements while suggesting a precise diet, to see how the body reacts to it. And there's huge variability from one person to another, and not just genetic.

Nothing grows from diamonds… The variability is linked to the microbiome, the microbes in our gut. So the kit also includes a poop collector to send by courier (Bartolini, out of spite) so Zoe can correlate the microbiome with the physiological responses above.

Battery dead? No problem: recently, devices have been proposed that can transmit information without chips or batteries, using only smart materials.

💸 ff.35.3 Lots of money in digital medicine

Substack ↗

In 2021 startups raised an extraordinary amount of funding (which collapsed in 2022 due to far less favourable interest rates). A CB Insights study gives an overview.

The five health companies that received the most funding (3 billion in total out of 38 invested in the US):

Devoted Health (for finding health insurance); Medlinker (the site is in Chinese, so who knows); Magic Leap (advanced 3D headsets, with medical applications); Neumora Therapeutics (more targeted/customised brain drugs); Hinge Health (digital straps or bands for posture/movement problems)

💤 ff.35.4 Super-sleep and super-focus

Substack ↗

7 easy pieces to sleep well and stay focused:

(1) Get sunlight by going outside as soon as you wake up (pyjamas are fine) (2) Wait a couple of hours before your first coffee (3) Drink water as soon as you wake up (4) Train early in the morning (within 3 hours of waking) to boost productivity at the start of the day (5) Practise partial fasting, with low carbs (but… I'm starving!) (6) Practise non-sleep deep rest for 30-90 minutes when physiology (and digestion) slow productivity down, especially in the early afternoon. (7) Minimise exposure to bright light between 22:00 and 4:00.

🌳 ff.35.5 Leopardi's L'infinito according to AI

Substack ↗

Nature is another fundamental part of feeling good. I think because of the relationship

nature = outdoors = more light

Being outdoors, daylight inputs are well separated from the artificial ones of indoor spaces, and our body manages to restore important circadian rhythms (we talked about it in 🎼 ff.22 Do we eat too much?)

Nature, greenery, also keeps us away from screens and social media. That makes us feel good too.

If you can't find “green all around”, don't worry. Artificial Intelligence can recreate it for us. I fed passages of Leopardi into Wombo Art. I find some of the AI's artistic interpretations of the world-famous poem fascinating.

e 'l naufragar m’è dolce in questo mare.
