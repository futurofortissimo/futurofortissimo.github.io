😴 ff.47.1 Snoring? No problem!

Substack ↗

Let's start with a bang. One of the most innovative gadgets at CES is Motion Pillow. A pillow with a built-in airbag that inflates when needed. So you won't have to spend the night gently elbowing your other half.

Snorlax has been warned. According to the group's super-detailed research, “snoring time” is almost halved.

🏆 ff.47.2 Digital sea serpents

Substack ↗

Every year CES awards the best technologies presented. This year, robots swept the board.

Are we made of 4 litres of water? No. But that's how many litres are lost in pipe leaks (this per capita average doesn't make much sense, given the huge differences in water consumption between countries. Turkmenistan leads, btw).

ACWA Robotics presented a robotic snake able to perform the equivalent of a colo... scopy to find water leaks and optimise their management.

🐧 ff.47.3 Washing machines against microplastics

Substack ↗

As is well known by now, besides panettoni (with or without candied fruit) we eat quite a lot of plastic microparticles every year.

MICROPARTICLES IN OUR BODY |50,000 eaten | 100,000 inhaled | 90,000 drunk (plastic bottle) | 4,000 drunk (tap water)

How do you dress? Before writing this article, I imagined microplastics came only from plastic waste broken down by the weather. Actually, microplastics come (35%) from our laundry in the washing machine.

At CES, Patagonia's Vincent Stanley presented the Less Microfiber Cycle, a wash programme able to cut the plastic released in the washing machine by 54%. Oh, we'd talked about textile sustainability: 🎼 ff.39 How do you dress?

A study quantifying microplastic release for various washing machine cycles offers more insights:

Say no to “Fast Fashion”; Wash less, colder, on a gentle cycle (70% of the reduction from this alone); Consider a microfiber filter

🧻 ff.47.4 Smart toilets

Substack ↗

Riddle: what is this contraption below?

Daddy, I need to pee. In previous episodes we talked about how we're having fewer and fewer babies and how we win Olympic titles by analysing what we, er, let out (this digital book helps you navigate the content).

CES couldn't do without a smart toilet (yes, there's a subtle pun in the Italian sentence before this one). Withings presented the U-Scan, a pee sensor to monitor:

The LH hormone (Luteinizing Hormone), linked to the menstrual cycle and ovulation; The specific gravity of the liquid, and so the user's hydration level; The pH, linked to the protein/fiber ratio

👍 ff.47.5 The “most fortissimo” tech gadgets

Substack ↗

Beyond these peculiar gadgets for everyone's health, CES also showed off a couple of really lovely little things.

Lenovo presented the Yoga 9i, a laptop with two screens (launch price $2300)

Need for Speed. BMW, with its Dee, showed a car body able to change colour like a chameleon. Forget the “pearlescent” paint of Underground 2.
