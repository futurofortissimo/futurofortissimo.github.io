❓ ff.5.1 All that glitters is not green

Substack ↗

A petrol city car that pollutes less than a Tesla? A promising Dutch start-up, Lightyear One, shows that not all that glitters is gold, or green. In fact, small cars with traditional engines pollute less than a Tesla Model S over their entire life cycle.

Lightyear One, it must be said, is pushing engineering to the extreme to increase efficiency and range. If we take average efficiencies (range, costs, etc…), ARK estimates that Tesla is still 3 years ahead of its competitors in electric mobility.

Sam Korus@skorusARKARK created an electric vehicle performance index that considers a vehicle’s range, implied drivetrain efficiency, and overall cost.

🔋 ff.5.2 Duracell? No, Tesla

Substack ↗

Not just cars: with 3 GWh of capacity (25% of the total) Tesla controls a good chunk of the world's electricity storage potential. And they aim to reach 1,500 GWh (500x) by 2030.

Getting to these numbers will take a lot of lithium. Which we'll then have to dispose of. Last year, 17.6 GWh of electric batteries reached end of life. In 15 years, that will be 10 times as much. Roughly 200 Olympic swimming pools (counting only batteries in electric vehicles).

🚲 ff.5.3 Pedal assist

Substack ↗

During the pandemic, e-bikes outsold electric cars (500k bikes vs 250k cars in the United States).

La vita moderna è costellata di momenti di scoperta della mobilità: il primo viaggio in macchina da adolescente; il primo viaggio in treno, aereo o autobus, guardando il mondo dalfinestrino. In questo decennio, è sempre più probabile che quel momento sia un giro inaugurale su unae-bike, che si dice possa suscitare una gioia infantile, elettrizzante e liberatoria. John Surico,The Popularity of E-Bikes Isn’t Slowing Down(New York Times, 2021)

130 million in 3 years: a Deloitte report predicts that 130 million e-bikes will be sold worldwide between 2020 and 2023.

With an e-bike you pedal twice as much. The share of trips made on two wheels rises from 19 to 50% if the bike is electric. An increase - the authors argue - unrelated to the novelty factor.

🧱ff.5.5 Marchetti's wall

Substack ↗

Have you watched Squid Game? No? If you spend at least 30 minutes a day getting to and from work, you could have watched it 15 times this year alone. Or watched 80 films. On average in the United States (but I don't think Milan is much different), commutes are actually longer: an hour (one year and two months of your life sitting in a car).

A universal constant hour: in 1994, the Italian physicist Cesare Marchetti observed that ever since ancient Rome, one hour has been the maximum bearable limit per trip, before people change jobs or move closer. Give or take, over two thousand years the commute hour has stayed the same.
