✊ ff.55.1 How does it feel, inside a revolution?

Substack ↗

Before any other reflection, I recommend Lex Fridman's ever-timely podcast with the founder of OpenAI.

When is it a revolution? Hard to say, but when the efficiency of the best-paid job in the world improves by 50% overnight and there's growing convergence (ARK Invest's annual report) between AI and other technologies, maybe we're close.

The guillotine. With the steam engine we delegated physical activity; now we're handing mental activities and memories over to digital. Just as for an 18th-century farmer physical labour was everything and a tractor was unimaginable, if AI takes away our mental faculties, what human dimension will we discover?

Meditate, people, meditate! Gyms and Ironmans stimulate the sedentary bodies of most workers: no feudal lord ever dreamed of running an ultra-trail or doing an everesting. In the same way, mindfulness and meditation (already on the rise, and not by chance) could become ever more important. Perhaps humans will fall back on the irreducible: their consciousness.

But, but, but. Let's not take even consciousness for granted, since computers are already reconstructing what we're seeing.

🌾 ff.55.2 Fields to plough (and data to reclaim)

Substack ↗

Sam Korus of Ark Invest points out how every revolution gives value to new raw materials and generates new outputs, citing Carlota Perez's book Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital.

Sam Korus@skorusARK1/From Carlota Perez's book:

Each new technological revolution is associated with new inputs.

-iron

-steam

-steel

-oil

-microelectronics

Is it finally time for data to take its place as the key input for the Age of AI revolution? 4:57 PM ∙ Feb 23, 2023226Likes54Retweets

agriculture → land holdings → private property; steam engine → means of production → industries; web/programming → computing power → websites and social media; neuromorphic algorithms → data ownership → metaverse (?)

Cookies and Cambridge Analytica. The debate on ownership of personal data was already opened by the scandals of recent years. Now that Stable Diffusion generates images by “learning” from public content on the internet without giving credit, the issue has become even hotter.

Hence sites like StableAttribution, which identifies which images inspired the DALL-E of the moment, rendering unto Caesar what is Caesar's.

👑 ff.55.3 Forget Julius Caesar: the new emperors

Substack ↗

In 🎼 ff.52 Founding a Nation in your garage we talked about the growing centralisation of some services (and the problems that come with it). What struck me was the collapse in the number of employees needed to reach 1M in revenue at S&P 500 companies.

Soon we might have $1T companies made up of a single employee assisted by hundreds of robots/artificial intelligences.

After all, AI-powered influencers and network effects are already creating personal brands (Mr. Beast's burgers, Emily Ratajkowski's NFTs): an example of what Li Jin calls the Passion Economy. But will they survive the endless flood of content soon to be produced?
