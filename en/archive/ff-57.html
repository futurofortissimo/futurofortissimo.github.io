🔱 ff.57.1 Between Greek myths, philosophy and new religions

Substack ↗

In a few days I'll be on the start line of the Padua Marathon. It'll be my first official one, but during the red-zone lockdown, staying strictly inside my municipality, I'd already covered the distance by running 6 laps of my charming hometown, Gazzaniga.

Modern pilgrims. Running past the 78 statues of illustrious figures linked to the city in Prato della Valle, I'll probably reflect on the millennia-old history of this race (unless the cramps have already taken over).

Sport is part of my daily life, partly as a response to family trauma. It can help fight depression and become a new religion. The members of this sect speak in code (VO2 max, BPM). They greet each other mid-run with reverence, as if they were priests.

Corriamo perché gran parte della vita è frustrante e futile, e solo contrastandola con qualche sorta di dolore, che richiede tempo, fatica e mesi di sforzi ripetitivi - lontani da amici, famiglia, birra e sanità mentale - siamo in grado di fare un atto di sfida alle Moire che complottano sulla nostra vita. Corriamo perché ci permette, seppur brevemente, di entrare in un altro mondo. Spesso questo può comportare disagio o addirittura dolore, una sensazione di alienazione dalla nostra normale zona di comfort che ci ricorda la nostra mortalità e ci fa rispettare le nostre capacità e limiti. Correre è l’equivalente laico del servizio domenicale. La maratona è l’equivalente odierno del pellegrinaggio medievale. Jamie Doward,Running in this London marathon is a way of dealing with my mum's death

A Futuro Fortissimo was due on this activity from the distant past, to rattle off the more or less beneficial effects of this simple, repetitive sequence of “long, well-stretched strides”.

👨‍⚕️ ff.57.2 Doctor's advice

Substack ↗

If you can't run a marathon, your health is shaky. That's the provocation on Twitter from Alan Couzens, an expert performance coach. Replying to a doctor who claims marathons are among the most pointless activities known to humanity, he points out:

Alan Couzens@Alan_CouzensUnpopular opinion:

If you're not fit enough to run a marathon right now (without negative consequences), you're not fit enough. Dr. Eli David@DrEliDavidUnpopular opinion: Running marathons is the most pointless activity known to humanity. 7:03 PM ∙ Jan 28, 2023705Likes53Retweets

Remember that we should study the Tour de France to cure diabetes? Well, if you have a VO2 max of 50+, your risk of dying from any cause is 4 times lower. That's Alan's starting point. A VO2 max of 50+ means, for a 75kg person, running a marathon in 5 hours. That is:

42 km in 5 hours = 7 min/km = 8.5 km/h = 7 cal/min = 2100 kcal

VO2 what? VO2 max is the maximum oxygen uptake (ml/min/kg) a given person can sustain. Basically, the body's “horsepower”. The higher it is, the better your cardiovascular/metabolic fitness. One way to measure it is the Cooper test.

🐊 ff.57.3 Running a (half) marathon in Crocs

Substack ↗

For a few weeks now, Crocs have officially been worth more than Credit Suisse.

Financial crises and Fed policy have nothing to do with it. It's because Benjamin Pachev (also a Harry Potter lookalike) wears these clogs to finish half marathons in 1h12. 3.24 min/km. 17.5 km/h.

🪰 ff.57.4 Volare, oh-oh

Substack ↗

Lacking Harry Potter's fitness, for Sunday's race I bought the AIR ZOOM ALPHAFLY NEXT% (without the 2, because I'm already poor enough). They're practically the ones Kipchoge used in Vienna to smash the two-hour barrier, and they give you a solid boost (probably 5 s/km, i.e. 3 minutes 30 seconds over a marathon).

Wind in your face. Siemens studied (with simulations) the previous world record set in Monza. Pacers and the lead car gave an overall gain of 7.3W, which translates into 4'35'' off the marathon time. More than the shoes.

Last year David Kilgore won the World Marathon Challenge: 7 marathons in 7 days on 7 continents.
