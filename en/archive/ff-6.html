The perfect pasta, easier breastfeeding and Amazon's elves

🎁ff.6.1 100 gifts to put under the tree

Substack ↗

If you're still looking for something to put under the tree this Christmas, Time has published a list of the 100 best inventions of the year that (despite everything) has just gone by.

Seriously: has anyone realised that 2021 has star… ended?

Among these inventions, Mobiliuslab's communication system allows safe conversation in high-risk situations, like construction sites. It doesn't need special microphones or earpieces: the sound literally travels through your bones.

Since augmented reality is on everyone's lips, Lenovo thought of offering us an unlimited number of screens with its ThinkReality A3 smart glasses. Or almost.

Not just productivity. Sfoglini, a pasta brand unknown to most but not to Americans, came up with a new shape of our favourite food, engineering every aspect of it, like "forkability",

firmness (adding right angles), but also increasing the variety of textures, with fluttering structures that at first glance look like an insect from Bear Grylls's diet.

In short, now you know what pasta to cook for the Christmas Eve feast (no, not the insect).

If instead you need to feed not just yourselves but also a little one, note that Elvie has released a breast milk pumping system. The purpose is noble: giving more freedom to women dealing with breastfeeding; the result, though, can't help but make you smile and recall something out of science fiction.

Adidas and Allbirds pre-released 100 pairs of a special shoe: one that emits, counting every aspect of production and shipping, 2.94 kg of CO2-equivalent (60% less than standard). As long as you don't wash them while using them*.

The future also runs through these 100 objects.

🧝ff.6.2 Amazon's elves

Substack ↗

Obviously, to make sure every parcel is delivered by the 25th under the trees of billions of people, Santa Claus gets help from Amazon. Which in turn gets help from 150 thousand seasonal workers, hired over the holidays. Practically double all of Nike's employees.

🛒ff.6.3 The advent of shopping, around the world

Substack ↗

Every holiday has its spending. And if holidays aren't enough, we create new ones: Black Friday, Cyber Monday, the New Year sales and, in Asia, created by Alibaba, Singles' Day (11 November, sigh).

Although Christmas, with its 1T (T = trillion = a thousand billion) dollars, is probably firmly in first place as the day we spend the most, it's interesting to see where the other days rank.

Sad? Shop! (:irony:) Cyber Monday (coinciding with the saddest day of the year, Blue Monday) has grown substantially in recent years. But with its 9.4 billion, it's only at 1% of Christmas spending.

In Asia, people spend to fight single-itude: in 2019 spending reached 38 billion. In 2021, 139 billion.
