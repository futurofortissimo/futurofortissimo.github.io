🤠 ff.60.1 The Westworld of self-driving: SF

Substack ↗

Are we there yet? Like many technological developments, self-driving has seemed ready to conquer the roads for years. Some trends start slowly, then become the norm in no time (see electric vehicles).

A child's dream is to go to San Francisco. Like Westworld in the HBO series, San Francisco is a parallel amusement park, a window onto the future. I wish some of these experiments happened closer to home. Instead, we get the Privacy Watchdog and the ChatGPT saga.

Cruise (General Motors' self-driving unit) launched its plan for a 24/7 robotaxi service. Self-driving is here (with Baidu and Waymo too). But the numbers are insignificant. And since only Tesla has scaled production consistently, we're waiting on them for the big leap in adoption.

⚡ ff.60.3 More money for taxis, less for carmakers

Substack ↗

There's a section on self-driving in ARK Big Ideas 2023 too (yes, despite the rejection after 5 interviews with them, I'm particularly fond of Cathie).

Fewer vehicles sold, but used more. That's the trend ahead of us. It won't make sense to buy a car and leave it parked 96% of the time. So self-driving will make more money for the owners of the taxi service than for the companies building the vehicles.

💀 ff.60.5 Fewer deaths on the roads?

Substack ↗

A road accident can pollute as much as 10 years of an average European's emissions (⛲ ff.58.3 Going to Niagara Falls (from New York)).

But are accidents on the rise? Honestly, I thought so (distracted by smartphones; data below only goes up to 2015, though). But if we normalise deaths per million inhabitants or per miles driven, fewer people die on the road.

Here are deaths per 100,000 people (including motorcyclists, pedestrians and cyclists) for some developed countries.

Better autonomous? On Tesla's website there's a quarterly summary of miles driven before an accident. From 500k (US average) it goes up to 4-6M (a 10x improvement).

California collects all the accident reports involving self-driving cars (111 in 2021). On the streets of San Francisco, in the same year, 37 people died (drivers, pedestrians and cyclists) out of 13,000 accidents.
