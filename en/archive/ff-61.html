⚖️ ff.61.1 Measuring pollution in hours of life

Substack ↗

We saw (🎼 ff.58 Do bananas pollute too much?) that Mike Berners-Lee proposes 10 tonnes of CO2 a year as a global per-capita target (i.e. a 50% cut for the average European).

If I don't measure, I don't know. With a CO2 “salary” to spend each month, and clear costs on every activity, maybe we could hit the target. So let's break that number down.

10 t/year → 27 kg/day → 1 kg/h → 20 g/min

1kg CO2/h. In practice, if an activity keeps us busy longer than its CO2-to-time conversion, it's green. For example, a banana is 4 minutes of pollution. And I usually eat it, and it fills me up, for longer than that.

A detailed table with the emissions of practically everything (taken from the book). Below, 10 examples (in minutes/hours) of the conversions we just talked about.

04 | banana06 | 100g rice18 | porridge with milk18 | pint of beer22 | daily emails30 | one km by car32 | egg60 | bottle of wine2h | hamburger2h | one km by car (in traffic)

If we take these numbers and factor in the quantity and frequency of a given activity, we get a cumulative analysis over the year.

Above, countless other contributions are missing (household consumption, building a house, mortgage, healthcare, other food). In short, there's little room for frugality, short of near-total, unrealistic renunciations at scale.

🥩 ff.61.3 Meat or asparagus?

Substack ↗

Being vegan is respectable: both for the environment and for a person's health (🍔 ff.49.3 Diets and pollutants).

But, but, but. I don't like the all-or-nothing approach of the vegan diet. Moderate meat consumption can be acceptable (one extra month of emissions in the 10-tonne model; with today's European average of 15 t, we're talking about just two weeks).

Diet profile | kgCO2/day | t CO2 per yearLots of meat | 7. 2 | 2. 62Little meat | 4. 5 | 1. 70Fish only | 3. 9 | 1. 42Vegetarian | 3. 8 | 1. 39Vegan | 2. 9 | 1. 05

-65 km by car a week. Cutting travel by this amount has the same impact as switching from a partly carnivorous diet to a fully vegan one.

Oh, and the vegan diet has its pitfalls too: asparagus.

🎶 ff.61.4 A Spotify-style subscription for the world

Substack ↗

Charming as its bucolic, back-to-nature narrative may be, happy degrowth isn't compatible with the digital first world.

Offsetting and believing in progress. As we saw in 📉 ff.58.5 Happy degrowth or tech growth?, one solution is installing solar panels or wind turbines. An ever cheaper solution.

futuro fortissimofuturo fortissimo on Instagram: ”🥳 Questi grafici sono quelli che fanno sperare nel futuro! Forza, energia solare, sky is the limit!”May 25, 2023

MyClimate helps offset our emissions. For this summer's return trip to Cuba I'll emit 2.7 t, corresponding to €76 (against a ticket that cost 950).

Polluting the world as if it were Spotify. Just as we live in a house and pay (through taxes) for healthcare and education, maybe we should start thinking of pollution as a Spotify subscription for using the world (and polluting it).

The 10 t a year cost €280. No excuses.
