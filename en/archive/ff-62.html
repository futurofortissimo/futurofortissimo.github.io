🔥 ff.62.1 Work has never been so important

Substack ↗

With the pandemic interruption breaking our habits, we also started questioning work (and its routine), perhaps realising how arbitrary certain paradigms are.

Only 40% of Americans. So 100 million people have a job taking up more than 35 hours a week. The “9-to-5” employee (if you're lucky) isn't the norm. It never was. For millennia, Weber's view of work prevailed: working to maintain the standard of living you've reached.

Copy-paste of our parents. The growth of the last 70 years may be unrepeatable. The growth inherent to the economic boom forged a genuine religion of work (and I live in Bergamo).

Everyone worked happily, in exchange for an ever better standard of living. Until today. Copy-pasting our parents, for social, demographic and economic reasons (🤖 ff.36.4 Are we already blended with AI?, a cost of living less sustainable for new generations), no longer seems to work.

🥾 ff.62.2 Leaving the beaten path

Substack ↗

In The Pathless Path, Paul Miller tells the story of his “escape” from the beaten path.

Studia. Prendi bei voti. Ottieni un buon lavoro. Poi, testa bassa e continua ad andare avanti, a chiedere di più, indefinitamente. Questo, ilDefault Path. Paul Miller,The Pathless Path

Paul Miller was the copy-paste poster boy, with stellar degrees and respectable career leaps (GE, McKinsey, Florida, New York). But then came the reflection:

Tutto andava bene, anzi era in continuo miglioramento. Eppure, ogni mattina la mia motivazione di andare al lavoro diminuiva, in un andamento inverso alla mia crescita di carriera e di stipendio.

32, Taiwan and an unhoped-for marriage. So he decided to drop everything, embracing an uncertain future of occasional freelance gigs. He then moved to Taiwan, where he met his wife, even though by then he'd written himself off as old, a hopeless single.

🕹️ ff.62.5 Independence and curiosity

Substack ↗

What's the solution?

On one hand, more flexible hours and remote working can help (freelancers and occasional jobs have grown from 10 to 15%; the WSJ reports workers are happier, maybe because they smoke more marijuana?).

Miller proposes creativity: finding an enjoyable activity to do, regardless of everything. For him, writing. Did it bring him JK Rowling's money and success? No. But that's not the point.

By writing, Miller finds motivation and builds connections with his readers. Belonging, esteem, self-actualisation. There's Maslow's pyramid again.
