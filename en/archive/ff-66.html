📙 ff.66.1 The central dogma

Substack ↗

Biology is complex: a cluster of ever larger atoms (molecules, proteins, RNA, DNA, lipid bilayers, one after another, “alla fiera dell'Est” style).

Before diving into today's 🎼ff, let's at least go over the main rules of the game.

Conosco le leggi del mondo, e te ne farò dono. Supererò le correnti gravitazionali,lo spazio e la luce per non farti invecchiare. Franco Battiato,La Cura

First the egg, then the chicken. Biology's central dogma says proteins are made following instructions from RNA, which in turn is a “temporary copy” of a stretch of DNA.

DNA → RNA → proteins

In therapy, too, we have three levels of action: modifying DNA, injecting RNA (as with vaccines) or working on protein design.

The good news? We're making progress on pretty much all of them:

DNA: Prime Editing. RNA: 😷 ff.53.3 From COVID to cancer. Proteins: 🧬 ff.4.1 The structure of proteins. Drugs and molecules: the first clinical trials of AI-discovered molecules. Basically, a MidJourney for the next paracetamol.

❓ ff.66.2 Are we getting sicker?

Substack ↗

Partly due to COVID, life expectancy in many countries has stalled (if not sharply collapsed), with the United States overtaken by China!

So the future of medicine doesn't look fortissimo, especially when you consider that:

Higher average life expectancy is also linked to the collapse in infant mortality (or is it?). Healthcare spending (US) has exploded over the last 50 years

Wait, wait, wait. It's not that things haven't improved. There's undeniably been a decrease in deaths from cardiovascular disease, and progress in cancer treatment is evident too (🤞 ff.53.1 Fewer cancer deaths).

But since 2012 annual progress has slowed, or even flatlined and reversed, as with cardiovascular disease.
