🐱ff.69.1 From Schrödinger's cat to qubits

Substack ↗

The idea for this newsletter came from Google's recent article: Sycamore-70 beats Frontier.

Frontier is the biggest “classical” supercomputer (in Tennessee). Compared to the previous Sycamore-54, in two years Google achieved an improvement of 7 orders of magnitude (10,000,000x)!

In practice, one second of quantum computing is now comparable to a year of “classical” computing on the world's biggest supercomputer. Clear?

Don't touch the cat (Schrödinger's): it can help us understand quantum computers. Until we measure (disturb) the system, the atoms (even those making up the giant feline) are “undefined”.

And what does this have to do with the supercomputer? Let's use the Financial Times illustrations. To solve a maze, we have to try a series of <1> (turn right) and <0> (left). As the forks multiply, we end up with an exponential number of possibilities.

That's in the “classical” world, the world of “big things”, of Newton's billiard balls.

An atom (qubit, quantum bit), on the other hand, can represent a superposition of <1|0>, <right|left>.

Like the cat that is <alive|dead> before we open the box and check on it (disturbing the wave function with an experimental measurement).

Simplifying a lot. Since each qubit represents both-right-and-left at the same time (until we measure), we don't have to program every combination of <right>/<left> to find the way out. That's all (bit).

🧼 ff.69.2 Quantum foam

Substack ↗

Another quantum consequence is, as we've seen, that the void doesn't exist (🔦 ff.64.1 The exhibition on the void). In fact, if an area were completely empty, we could say there's no matter (or energy, E=mc2) there. But saying the energy is exactly zero goes against Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

The void is a quantum foam. As in On Space Time Foam by Tomás Saraceno (2013, Hangar Bicocca). A project between art, the Big Bang, string theory and human beings, just the way we like it on 🎼ff.

🍏 ff.69.3 The quantum Apple?

Substack ↗

Rigetti Computing is trying to take quantum technology commercial (it recently went public on the Nasdaq, NASDAQ: RGTI -85% since trading began).

The pandemic had given a big boost to start-up/venture investment, with its push towards digital (for example, TDOC, which offers remote medicine). 2022 brought something of a return to earth (VC from $170B to $70B).

Not for quantum technologies, which saw private investment grow by $2.35 billion (McKinsey's Quantum Technology Monitor 2023).

The 10 companies that received the most investment. Which will be the quantum NVIDIA?

🤔 ff.69.4 What uses and dangers?

Substack ↗

But what are the applications of this technology, and the potential dangers?

Improving traffic in the port of San Francisco (SavantX). Monitoring brain scans to understand autism (SandboxAQ). Destroying VISA, Mastercard and maybe even Bitcoin

Bitcoin and VISA in danger? Yes, because the cryptographic systems underpinning today's financial system (and Bitcoin, Elliptic Curve Cryptography) can be solved (like the maze) in a finite and ever shorter time (Shor's algorithm).

If computers that you build are quantum,Then spies of all factions will want 'em. Our codes will all fail,And they'll read our email,Till we've crypto that's quantum, and daunt 'em. Peter Shor,Nobel per la Fisica 2023 proprio per studi su questi temi
