🌴 ff.70.1 The end of summer

Substack ↗

Since the autumn equinox is around the corner, let's sing one last hymn to summer and to the sun, which we'll be seeing less and less of in the coming months.

Quando il viso tuo nerissimoTornerà di nuovo pallidoQuesti giorni in riva al marNon potrò dimenticarEdoardo Vianello,Abbronzatissima

Breaking news. Beyond the fact that TG5 has been sensationalising for decades, this summer really was, statistically, hot.

So much so that Niall Ferguson, in a Bloomberg piece, writes an obituary for beach tanning, killed off by unbearable heat. Will the week on the Riviera meet the same fate as ski weeks in the Alps?

P.S. Personally, in August I was in Cuba. Crazy temperatures and humidity. But the problems there are more primal: getting your daily bread. If you want to dig deeper, I collected a few reflections in my very personal travel diary.

☢️ff.70.2 Oppenheimer: fission or fusion?

Substack ↗

Our atmosphere is getting hotter precisely because of the sun, obviously. But can't we channel this cluster of fused atoms in a positive, fortissimo way?

Oppenheimer helps ride the reopened debate on nuclear power. The latest novelty is Small Modular Reactors (SMRs), mini-reactors that produce 1/10 to 1/100 of the energy of their “bigger” siblings. They cost less and “spread” the (perceived!) risk of nuclear across a larger (and so less centralised) number of sites.

💣 ff.70.3 Solar panels are the bomb!

Substack ↗

Today, though, rather than the possible comeback of nuclear, I'm keen to talk about the latest developments in solar!

Since investment has shifted from fossil fuels to solar panels:

today, the cost of the electricity they produce has become negative. Meaning you make money by installing them.

Exponential, Watson! What follows is an ever wider rollout, so much so that the International Energy Agency lists them among the 3 (out of 50) technologies on track for the 2030 goals.

☎️ ff.70.4 Energy, TVTTTBXS

Substack ↗

Remember when we only had 100 texts a day? Today we have practically unlimited gigs. Energy could meet a similar fate. If manufacturing and transport get electrified, everything will cost much less (forget 🌟 ff.50.4 Consumers, but not of energy).

We'll remember CO2 emissions like the abbreviations we typed on T9: TVTTBXS micmer

The end of energy stagnation shows in the rise of power generation capacity (stuck since the early 2000s, for lack of clean alternatives).

And this is happening in both the US and China. The world's two biggest economies.

In short, solar seems to be “exploding”. And atomic bombs have nothing to do with it. And with this message of hope, that's all for today. If you're still hot, take an (artistic) dip.
