♾️ ff.72.1 Digital infinity

Substack ↗

The anxiety of our digital society is fuelled by FOMO, fear of missing out. During COVID, I remember living on Reddit's r/covid page, refreshing it to track infection data for every micro-region on the planet and find out the latest R0.

Zetta… what? The zettabyte is the new standard unit for data. 10^21 bytes (that's 250 billion full HD films).

The ZB generated by humans from 2010 to 2025 (100x in 15 years).

With generative art, these numbers are bound to explode, but according to Wired we're not ready for this world drowning in AI.

In short, it goes without saying that we're bound to miss something.

💿 ff.72.2 Listening to a CD

Substack ↗

Analogue was simpler. As a kid, I'd buy a CD (for the price of a month of Spotify) and wring it dry for months in the car. I subscribed to Internazionale, which I read, distraction-free, cover to cover.

Today I can't listen to a whole album on Spotify even once. Unless the artist, like Mike Posner, explicitly asks me to in Introduction.

A little exercise. To slow down a bit, I suggest listening to A Real Good Kid all the way through. As in Greek tragedies, the album gives voice to the very human suffering of those whom society (and social media) makes us see as “invincible heroes”. Among them, the tragedy of Avicii, a dear friend of the artist.

📚 ff.72.3 If the Library of Alexandria had 100 books

Substack ↗

Too many books? According to Naval, yes (from the podcast mentioned here: 🎁 ff.68.4 Less ego and control, more flow and presence: Naval's cure). So much so that people end up reading dozens of personal finance books without ever leafing through Adam Smith's The Wealth of Nations, for example.

Back to the classics. Pick 100 books and understand them deeply: that's the provocation from illacertus on X.

🇬🇷 ff.72.4 From multitasking to the Greek classics (and arete)

Substack ↗

As with books and CDs, we need to extend the philosophy of ONE to our whole being: one screen, one tab, one activity, one thought.

La persona che si concentra su un compito e lo porta a termine, anche se lavora in modo un po' lento o obsoleto, batte l'eterno ottimizzatore che salta da uno strumento all'altro e spera sempre che un nuovo pezzo di tecnologia lo aiuti a completare ciò che ha iniziato. James Clear,3-2-1: On seizing the day, perseverance, and focusing on one task at a time

So we come to the third nod to Greek classicism, a pillar of our humanist culture.

Nell'antica Grecia, una virtù morale primaria era l’arete(ἀρετή), l'eccellenza raggiunta nell'applicazione della presenza completa nel proprio mestiere. Brad Stulberg,The Practice of Groundedness

And that's all for today. Remember, UNO colour at a time (or red-colour-change).
