⚡ff.75.1 Tesmed for the head

Substack ↗

Remember when, between an episode of Dragon Ball and Naruto, they'd show us Tesmed ads? No? Let me unlock a memory for you.

Now, in a decidedly more scientific way, people are trying to do exactly the same. But with the brain.

Trans…what? With surgically implanted electrodes we've made giant strides against Parkinson's, but transcranial stimulation can be less invasive. These are devices that, placed on the outside of the skull, help with post-stroke rehabilitation, as motor support or as a treatment for depression!

If you want to dig deeper: review and market forecasts (N1, the first device from Musk's Neuralink, could generate $200M by 2030, and clinical trials have started for patients in certain categories).

😴ff.75.2 Neural lullaby

Substack ↗

Thanks in part to AI, we're learning to read thoughts, understand sleep, measure consciousness. On that note, an embarrassing video of micmer straight from 2019.

If the video wasn't enough and you don't have a Jigglypuff handy, relax, there's Somnee.

It uses transcranial stimulation, that very one, to promote sleep (scientific principle). Basically, the device shifts the distribution of brain waves, bringing it closer to the one linked to a sleep state.

Like a relaxing massage, but for the brain.

↑ β-waves → wakefulness ↓ α-waves → sleep

Insomnia, er, in sum. Better than melatonin!

👮ff.75.4 Thought police

Substack ↗

In his book Exponential, Azeem Azhar discusses the chasm opening up between regulation and technology. Neural chips throw more fuel on the fire already stoked by AI, social media, robotics and climate.

He talked about mind control with Nita Farahany (professor emerita at Duke University, North Carolina).

A few reflections:

Collecting brain data, a growing interest with $1 billion in investment. The possibility of monitoring (and modifying) thoughts (see Orwell: thoughtcrime and 1984). The need to open a discussion on mental privacy

And that's all. I'm off to stimulate my brain. With some bubble tea.
