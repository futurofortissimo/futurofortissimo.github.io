🤑ff.77.3 Or of money?

Substack ↗

Pope Francis argues it's more of an economic issue. But with what I spend on Michelin-starred dinners, trips to the other side of the world and Ironman entries, I could probably support a child.

It looks like “money” is just an excuse.

Or is it? Housing, the expense that weighs most on young people's lives and the conditio sine qua non for having a child, has recently become a problem.

But over the last decade affordability was far higher than in periods like the '80s. So this doesn't seem to be the main reason.

🛠️ff.77.4 Or of emancipation?

Substack ↗

Does the career woman have no time for kids? It's not an easy question, so much so that this year it earned Harvard's Claudia Goldin the Nobel Prize in Economics.

The fall in fertility doesn't seem tied only to the rise in working women. At the start of the 1900s, true, almost no women worked. But in the 1800s, 60% of women were in the fields with their husbands.

After giving birth, new generations of mothers are more and more often going back to work and taking on higher positions.

1️⃣ff.77.5 Or an individualistic choice?

Substack ↗

So where's the answer? Maybe we're simply more individualistic?

Society pushes us to optimise every aspect of life (🎼 ff.68 Such anxiety! (pt. 2)). Maybe, in our individualistic priorities, we simply decide to stop looking for a partner.

My take. We're in a new world compared to that of the 1800s and the baby boomers, with new and unexplored assumptions about values, money and society (number of children, of homes owned, of families under one roof)
