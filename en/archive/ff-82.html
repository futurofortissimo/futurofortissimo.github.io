🦖ff.82.1 Mass extinction?

Substack ↗

500 million years ago, the Cambrian explosion. Perhaps a new organism able to produce oxygen “suffocated” the other life forms that lived by breathing CO2.

Today, CO2 is scary again. AI-driven emissions are growing: one exchange with ChatGPT equals an hour of LED light, one DALL-E image a smartphone charge.

Will computronium be the next form of life? Will CO2 get its revenge?

But, but, but. Doom and sensationalism aside, here on 🎼ff let's try to dig a little deeper into the question:

Comparing ChatGPT to LED light is misleading. In one case we're talking about the first example of a mass technology, in the other the result of a century of improvements (compared to the tungsten filament). Even if ChatGPT uses 10x a Google search, how many other Google searches does it prevent?

What's more, MIT points out that AI's energy consumption can bring benefits and that efficiency solutions already exist:

rolling out Small Nuclear Reactors (Microsoft's proposal), optimised management of computing resources

Nerd corner. The report on projections of internet energy consumption.

🐔 ff.82.2 Computational chickens

Substack ↗

My cock is much bigger than yoursMy cock can walk right through the doorSystem of a Down,Cigaro

With this analogy, System of a Down tackle the arms race. Data centres could be the next “cock”, the next rockets, the next trenches.

True, smaller and more efficient models are spreading (see Mistral, the French startup that scares OpenAI), with results comparable to the Goliaths GPT-4 and Gemini. But as parameters (and computing power) scale up, results keep getting better.

Good. It's not a given to have a technology that improves simply by throwing more resources at it: see the “S-curve” innovation of wind turbines and combustion engines.

🥵 ff.82.4 Hot war

Substack ↗

In 2024 NVIDIA will produce 1M H100 GPUs ($40k each, with 9x the performance of the A100).

Noam Shazeer, CEO of Character.ai, calculates that we have enough GPUs to run one GPT-4 model per person. But there isn't computronium for everyone, and the war is above all between companies.

Tesla grabbed only 15k GPUs. Even OpenAI (despite the Microsoft partnership) doesn't have all the resources it needs (maybe one of the reasons behind Altman's ousting, later reversed?).

Visionary as ever, Musk is building his independence with Dojo, the tailor-made supercomputer that in 2024 will be in the world's top 5 (analysis by Sawyer Merritt).

David among the Gol-AI-ths (sorry). Columbia researcher Ishan Dhanani wants to democratise access to computronium by decentralising it.
