🧮ff.84.1 Old screens?

Substack ↗

05535 0705 (if you know what I mean). We all typed this number on a calculator's LCD. Liquid crystal displays are also behind Esselunga's ever more astronomical prices.

This '90s invention could be the most interesting novelty of CES2024. Let's see why.

No wires, no batteries. Ambient Photonics develops solar cells that capture a room's natural light. Built into a mouse or gaming headphones, they let you use them with no batteries and no wires.

Given how little power LCDs use, the two technologies combine in new battery-free smart devices. Like this alarm clock that tells you how your GameStop investments are doing (badly).

🦪ff.84.4 Molluscs and other sensors

Substack ↗

Physical objects will be more and more online: by 2030 the Internet of Things will count 30 billion connections, 3x today's figure.

Ostriche e champagne, viene giù una valangaStriscio lo ski pass, settimana biancaIl Pagante,Settimana Bianca

Among them, molluscs? MolluSCAN connected oysters to the cloud to monitor their health. The molluscs “returned the favour” and turned into sensors useful for predicting heatwaves and monitoring climate change.

Sensor quality isn't everything, thanks to AI. The latest vision models can identify people from just a few pixels.

Or, less dystopically, air pollution using the 1MB cameras of retired smartphones.

One last sensor: the X-ray camera from Otom, a Korean company that photographs your wisdom teeth. The prototype, mercifully for CES visitors, wasn't working.

So no need for diets: in your beach photos this summer you'll be skeletal.
