‍💨 ff.87.1 A short history of breathing

Substack ↗

Feuerbach (reviewing Jakob Moleschott's Popular Treatise) claimed that we are what we eat.

Food, chewing, breathing. This connection floored me: chewing less because of processed food, our palate has changed shape over the last two centuries, bringing problems like snoring (related: the effects of bottle-feeding versus breastfeeding on breathing and teeth alignment).

So are we what we breathe?

James Nestor explores the subject in the phenomenal Breath. A few takeaways:

Taping your mouth shut at night (or during workouts) leads to better sleep/performance. The amygdala, more than the brain's fear centre, looks like a CO2 sensor. So much so that the famous impossible-to-scare patient SM was terrified by a single breath of CO2. Email apnea is a thing: we're so caught up in notifications and multitasking that we forget to breathe (is that why sport, meditation or singing, which force us to breathe, prevent stress?).

In short, a book you should not read in one breath.

🔄 ff.87.3 An air purifier for everyone

Substack ↗

In futuro fortissimo 58 we talked about bananas and the cost of a human life.

Converted into (lousy) CO2 emission credits: €3,000 (🔟 ff.58.1 The cost of a human life).

To offset pollution, someone wondered whether it's worth buying an air purifier for every American.

DALYs? Disability-adjusted life years: the gain in expected years of life free of disability.

$33k. The hypothetical cost of adding 1 DALY to an American's life with an air purifier. In India, with sky-high PM2.5 and smaller homes, $1.5k.

A tad steep, considering that:

€65k. The cost of offsetting 20 years of a European's emissions by installing a wind turbine (📉 ff.58.5 Happy degrowth or tech growth?). Artemisinin for malaria comes in at €140/DALY.
