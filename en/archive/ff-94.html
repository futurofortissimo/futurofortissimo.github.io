🤰ff.94.1 How to get pregnant?

Substack ↗

In past issues we talked about obesity (🎼 ff.49 The pandemic of the 21st century). It's a delicate, tangled subject, just like the rest of human biology.

Stigmatising fat leads to social and psychological problems, like bulimia. Deborah Clegg explains why a bit of fat matters, especially on women's hips.

The highlights:

Fat cells are a source of oestrogen and regulate ovulation, so much so that without them a woman's cycle and fertility shut down. The fat that builds up after menopause is in fact a “third ovary”, rebalancing the missing oestrogen. Soy and sprouts contain substances similar to “ovarian” oestrogens and can help fight the effects of menopause. Good and bad fat cells: the “white” ones are flexible and release the fat inside them more easily. The waist-to-hip ratio identifies obesity better than BMI.

Fast food, then? Not so fast: eating at McDonald's and eating little fruit lower fertility. During pregnancy and afterwards, though, better to overdo it a little: breastfeeding a baby takes up to 400 kcal a day.

🍔ff.94.2 Good fats and bad fats

Substack ↗

A bit of fat is good for you. But which kind?

Food and diet are a tangle of chemistry, biology and physiology. The nutrition facts on a food label aren't enough. Especially for fats.

Compound Interest sums up the 5 categories of fat:

Trans fats are to be avoided: the rise in cardiovascular disease. Thanks to their C=C double bonds, polyunsaturated fats can have antioxidant properties and lower cholesterol.

Oil Pokédex. Flaxseed oil is “super effective” in omega-3s (but the short ones, read below). Because of its high saturated fat content, on the other hand, better steer clear of the “coconut-bello”.

As I said, though, there's a lot of chemistry involved. Temperatures and reagent ratios have to be precisely controlled. If you took up home baking during lockdown, you know.

So much so that:

If we overheat “good” oil (extra virgin, polyunsaturated) we turn it into trans fat, silently doing ourselves a lot of harm (all it takes is “flipping” the C=C bond in the carbon chain). Polyunsaturated fats are good for you, but too many omega-6s compared to omega-3s maybe not.

To dig deeper (it's long, I warn you), the Dynomight blog.

🚬ff.94.3 Omega-3s like cigarettes?

Substack ↗

OK, I've thrown more meat on the fire and the question “which fats?” is even more tangled.

A simple answer, if you have to pick which fats to take: “long” omega-3s.

In this phenomenal podcast (we'll come back to it), Rhonda Patrick points out:

Taking 1g of omega-3s a day (50g of salmon) can have the same effects as quitting smoking. The biggest benefits come from “long” omega-3s (=EPA and DPA), found in animal sources (fish). The “short” omega-3 variant (=ALA) found in seeds and vegetarian diets, even if partly “lengthened” during digestion, isn't enough. Vegetarian or pescatarian? To hit the target simply and consistently, it's worth taking a supplement (just one, promise, not like 💊 ff.49.4 The superman who takes 100 pills a day)
