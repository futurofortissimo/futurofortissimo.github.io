🔑ff.95.3 Digital private property

Substack ↗

That's how Chris Dixon of the A16Z Crypto fund makes the case for a new internet in his brand-new book Read Write Own.

What is web3? And above all, what are web1 and web2?

web 1 | read + open protocols like the ones still used today by email (SMTP) and websites (HTML) - systems with no organisation and no financial incentives for users web 2 | read and write + centralised databases and user-friendly platforms - free speech but no control over the means of distribution/algorithms web 3 | read, write and own + decentralised databases with blockchain (the web2 advantage) + immutable rules that reward user participation (with tokens) and make funding easier

A good summary of the book is the chat between the author and Balaji (ex-CTO of Coinbase, 🌐 ff.52.3 Founding your own nation).

✍️ ff.95.4 Reinventing writing and printing

Substack ↗

Writing and newsletters on Substack could be a first example of the shift to web3. Let's see why.

Since newsletters are emails, running on an open protocol as we've seen, Substack can't lock in its writers if they want to move to other services (a copy-paste of the email list is all it takes).

Gutenberg or Telepass? Writing sits at the heart of another analogy. Just as the printing press broke the Inquisition's grip, blockchain gives network neutrality back to users/influencers/brands, who are forced to pay more and more for the same traffic on social media.

⛓️ff.95.5 What does blockchain solve?

Substack ↗

Asking what blockchain solves is like asking: “what does steel solve?” Chris Dixon, Read Write Own

Beyond censorship and “marketplace taxes”, here are the weak spots of today's online world:

Boxing. AI packs content harvested online into boxes/FAQs/chats, taking monetisation away from the original sites. Deepfakes, ever more convincing, undermine the credibility of online content (🍝ff.85.4 Carbonara and cryptocurrencies, verifymedia)

and the opportunities:

Tokenisation. In gaming, mods are the order of the day (League of Legends is a remix of a Warcraft III map). Blockchain rewards user creativity and contributions in a predictable way (Edenhorde, the collaborative Lord of the Rings that gives value back to users)

Interoperability. Moving contacts, content and purchases from one platform to another with zero friction (as mentioned, see the Substack example) Bureaucracy. The example of Etherisc, which refunds your plane ticket in 10 minutes
