🌶️ ff.99.2 Polenta and spermidine

Substack ↗

Let's start with something “spicy”: spermidine can extend life by almost 6 years.

It's a polyamine found in peas (no coincidence), wheat, soy and polenta. Its chemical structure helps protect DNA.

Here's a table of the richest foods.

NSFW. Yes, the name doesn't lie. It's also found in human sperm. Each ejaculation, however, contains only 0.1 mg of spermidine. If you want something white, better go for tea, so protective of DNA that it can even be used to preserve sperm itself.

🥔 ff.99.3 Purple potatoes: the ultimate superfood?

Substack ↗

Instead of €100-a-jar supplements, Michael suggests plants, fruit and legumes. In short, another fine example of simplicity.

Whether it's sport, finance or diet, in life you don't need to invent anything complicated. All it takes is simple things, consistency, patience and, above all, not doing anything stupid.

💩 ff.99.4 The microbiome in your poop

Substack ↗

Gut wellbeing and fibre: a recurring theme on futuro fortissimo, 🦠ff.92.2 One more organ: the microbiome, 🎼 ff.17 Sport (and the people who poop it).

We are not what we poop: 75% of poop is made up of microbes from our microbiome.

So the more fibre we eat, the more we feed the microbiome, the more we go to the bathroom, the more we “bloom like a flower”.

De André was right.

PS. Fibre isn't just good for your gut: for every 7g of fibre there's a 9% reduction in heart disease.

🎵 ff.99.5 More fortissimo notes

Substack ↗

To wrap up, 3 more of the many gems I found in the book:

The spice fenugreek (trigonella) raises testosterone, but makes you sweat maple-syrup-scented sweat. Where can you buy it? Incredibly, reading the label on LIDL's curry, I discovered it's among the ingredients. Drinking a glass of soy milk a day reduces the probability of breast cancer by 12% (a cumulative effect for every 5g of soy protein). Rather than cutting calories or protein, to extend your life it's important to limit the amino acid methionine, which among other things unbalances the precursor of melatonin and serotonin: the effects of eating meat on sleep.
